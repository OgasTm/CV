# IRSA physics / dosimetry core: public datasets, reference data and open tools (verified 2026-11-20)

Module codes used in the tables:
- **M1** = detector-reading inversion to retained activity / distribution. M1a = patient geometry from CT; M1b = activity-distribution prior from PET or SPECT.
- **M2** = external dose-rate / cumulative-dose engine (receptor at a given distance, orientation and duration, with body attenuation and shielding). M2-NRC = NRC point-source baseline; M2-SA = semi-analytic model (line source × body-attenuation factor × measured T_eff); M2-MC = Monte Carlo reference.
- **M3** = MC training-data generation and AI surrogate.
- **M4** = uncertainty quantification (conformal prediction).
- **M5** = validation against measurements.
- **M6** = receptor body models (adult, child, pregnant family member).
- **M7** = nuclear and interaction data layer.

Verification method: dataset metadata (title, license, last update) was read from the DataCite REST API (api.datacite.org) for every DOI below, because Deep Blue pages sit behind a Cloudflare challenge. Software licenses were read from the LICENSE files on raw.githubusercontent.com and from PyPI JSON. Bibliographic facts were checked against the Crossref API. "Not verified" marks anything that could not be confirmed in this session.

## Q1. Public patient imaging datasets relevant to RPT (177Lu / 131I SPECT/CT, PSMA and SSTR PET, whole-body CT segmentation)

### Takeaway
The only open multi-time-point 177Lu patient SPECT/CT data are the 2-patient 177Lu-DOTATATE SNMMI Dosimetry Challenge set on Deep Blue (CC BY-NC 4.0). It covers projections, reconstructions, TIA maps, VOIs, planar whole-body scans and pre-therapy Ga-68 PET. For PSMA PET, PSMA-PET-CT-Lesions is large: 597 studies, CC BY 4.0 on TCIA, but CC BY-NC 4.0 on the Tübingen mirror. DEEP-PSMA (100 pre-177Lu-PSMA PSMA+FDG pairs, CC BY-NC) is also available. TotalSegmentator v3 (1,939 CTs, CC BY 4.0) is the best open source for patient geometry. I found no open 177Lu-PSMA SPECT/CT, no 131I SPECT/CT, no 225Ac imaging dataset and no sizeable public SSTR-PET dataset.

### Inferences
- All Dewaraja/Deep Blue patient data are CC BY-NC 4.0. They are fine for an IAEA TC research prototype, but a commercial IRSA product could not train on them without a separate agreement. The same holds for DEEP-PSMA and for the FDAT copy of the PSMA set.
- The open patient data cannot support a learned PSMA-PET→177Lu-PSMA prior. Two of the target pathways can be bridged now: the PSMA PET cohort (TCIA, CC BY) combined with TotalSegmentator organ masks and published 177Lu-PSMA organ kinetics; and the 2-patient 177Lu-DOTATATE set for an end-to-end 177Lu SPECT test.
- For the torso-worn detector inversion (M1), the planar whole-body time series (y4xd-s758) and the TIA maps give a ground truth from which to simulate what a body-worn detector would read at each of the 4 time points.

### Gaps
- No open 177Lu-PSMA multi-time-point SPECT/CT patient dataset was found. The FNIH PDIB project says it intends to publish multiple-time-point SPECT/CT datasets in future, but none are available yet ([JNM 2026;67(8):1198](https://jnm.snmjournals.org/content/67/8/1198)).
- No open 131I SPECT/CT patient dataset and no 225Ac imaging dataset were found in DataCite (Zenodo, Figshare, Deep Blue, Mendeley, institutional repositories).
- No downloadable dataset from an IAEA coordinated research project on RPT dosimetry was found. The IAEA PLANETDose QA study exists as a paper ([doi:10.60692/agrac-wn082](https://doi.org/10.60692/agrac-wn082)) and I found no linked data.
- File sizes and file counts of the Deep Blue records could not be read: the site returned a Cloudflare challenge to both automated fetch and curl.
- The DEEP-PSMA access mode (open or restricted on Zenodo) was not confirmed.

## Q2. Computational phantoms (reference adult, paediatric, BMI-varied, pregnant receptors)

### Takeaway
ICRP 110 (adult voxel), 143 (paediatric voxel) and 145 (adult mesh) are now free to download, but ICRP keeps copyright and reuse needs permission. ICRP 156 (paediatric mesh, 2025) is not yet free. The UF/NCI library is the main source of BMI-varied and pregnant phantoms: 362 body-size and 8 pregnant phantoms per the NCI manual, free for non-commercial research under an NCI Software Transfer Agreement. MIDA is head-and-neck only. XCAT is licensed for a fee by Duke.

### Inferences
- For M6 receptors, the shortest route is ICRP 110/145 adults plus ICRP 143 children (free to download now) and UF/NCI pregnant and BMI-varied phantoms (STA, non-commercial). Redistributing any ICRP phantom inside the app requires ICRP permission, so a deployed surrogate should ship only trained weights, not phantom files.
- ICRP 145 ships ready-made Geant4/PHITS/MCNP6 inputs. That makes PHITS or Geant4/GATE the cheapest way to get MC external-dose training data with posture-adjustable mesh bodies.

### Gaps
- The exact licence text for downloaded ICRP phantom files (beyond the general reuse statement) was not found.
- No ICRP or other open mesh phantom of a pregnant woman was found; only the UF/NCI series.
- No open-licensed (CC BY) family of BMI-varied whole-body phantoms was found. UF/NCI is non-commercial under an STA.
- XCAT whole-body licence fees are not public.

## Q3. Nuclear and interaction data, conversion coefficients, dose-rate constants and buildup factors

### Takeaway
All core reference data are freely accessible: ICRP 107 decay data (free publication; machine-readable copy in the CRAN package RadData), NNDC NuDat 3, IAEA LiveChart, NIST XCOM and ESTAR, ICRP 116 and ICRP 74 (free to download). ANSI/ANS-6.4.3 buildup factors are a paid, withdrawn standard, but the same data are distributed as RSICC DLC-129 / ORNL/RSIC-49/R1. NRC RG 8.39 Rev. 1 (April 2020) lists Γ for I-131 (2.2 R·cm²/mCi·h) but has no Lu-177 or Ac-225 row. Measured 177Lu patient dose rates are about half the bare-source Γ, which makes a body-attenuation factor essential.

### Inferences
- Unit bridge for the NRC baseline: 2.2 R·cm²/(mCi·h) for I-131 equals 0.22 mR/h per mCi at 1 m. Under RG 8.39's 1 R = 10 mSv convention that is about 2.2 µSv/h per mCi, or about 0.059 µSv·m²/(MBq·h). The theoretical 177Lu figure of 0.007636 mSv/h per GBq equals about 0.0076 µSv·m²/(MBq·h), roughly 8× lower than I-131. These are my conversions; check them against Smith & Stabin.
- Because RG 8.39 Rev. 1 has no 177Lu Γ, the IRSA "NRC default" baseline must take a literature Γ (for example Smith & Stabin, or the 0.007636 mSv/h/GBq used by Bellamy). That choice should be documented. The Bellamy BMI regression and the 48% mean ratio give a ready prior for the M2-SA body-attenuation factor.
- ICRP 116 handles fluence→E for the receptor; ICRP 74 gives H*(10), the quantity survey-meter validation data are reported in. Both are needed to compare the MC surrogate with the measurement tables in Q6.

## Q4. Open Monte Carlo and dosimetry software: licensing and suitability for external-dose training data

### Takeaway
For generating external-dose training data around a patient, OpenGATE 10 is the best fit: a Python API on Geant4, LGPL-3.0, PyPI 10.1.2 released 2026-11-09, and a ready `opengate_dose_rate` tool. The new radot toolbox (LGPL-3.0, by the GATE lead) wraps it for RPT. OpenMC (MIT) and OpenTOPAS (MIT-style) are permissive alternatives. PHITS is free and natively reads the ICRP 145 phantoms. EGSnrc is AGPL-3.0, which matters for any server-side deployment. Organ-level tools such as MIRDcalc (v1.26, Oct 2026), IDAC-Dose 2.1 and OpenDose are free but serve internal dose, not external.

### Inferences
- Validation can be layered:

  - 2 m time series and T_eff: Kurth, Schütze.
  - Continuous fixed-detector retention: Bailey 2026 for 131I, the nearest analogue to the wearable inversion problem.
