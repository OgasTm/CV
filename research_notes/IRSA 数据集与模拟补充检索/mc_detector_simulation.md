## Q1. Published MC and measurement studies (2015–2026, plus key earlier anchors) of dose rate vs distance, orientation and posture around 131I / 177Lu / 225Ac / 223Ra patients; self-shielding; effective dose vs H*(10)

### Takeaway
Close-contact geometry has been simulated with phantoms mainly for **131I**:
- Han et al. 2013 (UF hybrid phantoms) covers face-to-face exposure, **a mother holding her newborn** and a child on the patient's lap.
- Dewji et al. 2015 (ORNL PIMAL + MCNP6 + Leggett iodine biokinetics) covers standing and **seated public-transport postures**.
- Taghvaee et al. 2018 (MIRD phantoms) covers a **patient lying in bed** with a caregiver standing beside them.

For **177Lu**, the only phantom-MC study of the 3D field outside a patient found is Li et al. 2024 (PHITS, 177Lu-PSMA, adult male phantom, verified by measurement, rRMSD 0.21–0.25 within 1 m). No 177Lu child-holding or bed-sharing MC study was found, and no 225Ac/223Ra phantom MC study was found. None of these studies deposits code or data. Measured bed-sharing doses exist for 131I (TLD on a pillow), but only from search summaries. No paper gives an effective-dose / H*(10) ratio for RPT-patient bystander geometries; EURADOS lists this as future work.

**Self-shielding, 225Ac/223Ra and E vs H*(10)**
- 225Ac gamma lines come from daughters: 218 keV (221Fr) and 440 keV (213Bi). Emission probabilities are quoted as 11.6% and 26.1% in one source and 11.4% and 25.9% in another — [arXiv 2406.05220](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.05220); [Theranostics/PMC8041692](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8041692/)
- 223Ra principal gammas at about 81, 154 and 270 keV — [Theranostics 2021;11:9721](https://www.thno.org/v11p9721.htm)
- No phantom MC study of external dose around 225Ac or 223Ra patients was found (searches on PubMed/Europe PMC terms and web). The only measured data remain the J Nucl Med Technol 225Ac-lintuzumab/223Ra values already catalogued in the earlier notes.
- EURADOS-EANM (already catalogued) states that the validated framework "will be further used for the evaluation of effective dose rates per unit administered activity for a variety of close-contact configurations", with sex-averaged effective doses from ICRP male and female phantoms and the patient as the ICRP reference female — [Struelens et al. 2024, PMC11035505](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11035505). No follow-up paper with those coefficients was found up to 2026-11-23.
- Peplow 2020 defines its specific gamma-ray dose constant as **effective dose rate (ICRP 116, AP geometry) at 1 m** per unit activity, not air kerma or H*(10) — [OSTI 1607209](https://www.osti.gov/biblio/1607209). For 177Lu this gives 4.9–5.2 µSv·h⁻¹·GBq⁻¹ at 1 m (see Q4), against the 7.636 µSv·h⁻¹·GBq⁻¹ "theoretical" value used by Bellamy 2022 (earlier notes).

### Inferences
- The child-holding (Han 2013), seated (Dewji 2015) and bedside (Taghvaee 2018) geometries define an IRSA scenario set: face-to-face 0.1–3 m; side-by-side seated; seated behind; lying side by side 0.3 m; holding an infant against the chest; child on lap. All of it is published for 131I and none for 177Lu, so IRSA's M3 data generation would be the first 177Lu equivalent.
- Dewji's 131I overestimation factors (1.9–3.3 at 1 m; ≫10 at 0.1–0.3 m) and Al-Haj's 0.46 body-shielding factor play the same role for 131I as Bellamy's 48% for 177Lu. Together they bound the M2-NRC vs M2-SA gap.
- The 0.5 m/1 m dose-rate ratio of about 1.4–1.5 in Sheikhzadeh 2025 shows that at ≤1 m a point source with inverse-square falloff fails for 177Lu, not just because of body attenuation. A line or volume source (M2-SA) is required.
- Quantity choice matters by up to ~1.5× for 177Lu: effective dose AP (Peplow) vs an air-kerma/exposure-based Γ. IRSA must fix one quantity (recommend E for receptor dose, H*(10) for comparison with survey-meter validation data) and document it.

### Gaps
- No open code or data from any posture, bed-sharing or child-holding MC study was found.
- No 177Lu or 225Ac phantom study of child holding, bed sharing or seated postures was found.
- Han 2013 vs Dewji's description of it conflict: the Med Phys abstract says UF **hybrid** phantoms, while Dewji calls them "stylized phantoms developed previously by ORNL" and adds a side-by-side lying case. The two may describe different Han et al. 2013 papers, possibly "A practical guideline for the release of patients treated by I-131 based on Monte Carlo dose calculations for family members" ([ResearchGate 261375415](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/261375415_A_practical_guideline_for_the_release_of_patients_treated_by_I-131_based_on_Monte_Carlo_dose_calculations_for_family_members)), which Crossref did not resolve in this session (rate limited).
- The Dewji OSTI manuscript contains an internal inconsistency: one results sentence says the point source "underestimates" for hyperthyroid patients beyond 30 cm, while the conclusions say it "consistently overestimates" from 10 to 300 cm. Check the published version.
- No numerical E/H*(10) ratio for RPT bystander geometry was found.

## Q2. Posture-deformable and motion-capable computational phantoms and body models, with licences

### Takeaway
- **Hanyang University (C.H. Kim group)** leads posture deformation of the ICRP mesh phantoms:
  - Yeom et al. 2019 published posture-dependent dose coefficients (walking, sitting, bending, kneeling, squatting) using ARAP deformation driven by motion capture.
  - Han et al. 2025 (Health Physics) released MPPD, a GUI for posture plus stature deformation of MRCPs. Its download terms were not found.
- **ORNL/NRC PiMAL** is the only articulated-limb phantom tool with a public distribution path: GitLab landing page, NRC RAMP, v6 in 2025. It is tied to MCNP.
- For motion and body-shape data outside radiation physics:
  - SMPL/SMPL-X/AMASS are research-only; commercial use needs a paid licence, and some MPI licences forbid training commercial networks.
  - Only the **SMPL-Body subset (CC BY 4.0)**, **MakeHuman exports (CC0)** and **BodyParts3D (CC BY 4.0, per current README)** are usable in a product without negotiation.

### Inferences
- The cheapest posture-capable route for M3 is: ICRP MRCP (free download, reuse permission needed) + MPPD (if released) or Blender/ARAP deformation + PHITS or Geant4 tetrahedral geometry. PiMAL is fully articulated but locks you to MCNP and stylized anatomy.
- For a product: train on ICRP/UF-NCI phantoms but ship only weights; use MakeHuman (CC0) or SMPL-Body (CC BY 4.0) envelopes for any on-device body rendering or detector-placement UI. AMASS and full SMPL should be used only offline in research, since some MPI licences restrict training networks for commercial use.
- For M1, posture matters mainly through detector-to-source geometry (collar vs belt vs wrist as the patient sits or lies). Posture-deformed MRCPs carrying a 177Lu biodistribution give a direct way to simulate "same activity, different reading" artefacts. These are what Bailey 2026 saw for a ceiling detector (earlier notes).

### Gaps
- MPPD download and licence terms were not found.
- Yeom 2019 numerical posture effects were not retrieved (paywalled).
- PiMAL v6 licence text was not read (the GitLab API call timed out).
- No SMPL-based radiation phantom with organs and an open licence was found.
- No published posture-deformed **pregnant** or **child-holding** mesh-phantom pair was found.

## Q3. Simulating body-worn detector readings; detector response and angular dependence; open gamma-spectroscopy tools and datasets; Radiacode community data

### Takeaway
- **Patient-worn detector simulation:** published work is limited. It covers WIDMApp (already catalogued), the Iranian IRD bedside iodine dosimeter (MC-designed, fixed position) and the UW vest (earlier notes). No paper simulates a single CsI body-worn spectrometer on an RPT patient.
- **Detector response and analysis tools:** the open tooling is mature. Sandia InterSpec and SpecUtils are LGPL-2.1, Sandia PyRIID is BSD-3, LBNL becquerel is BSD-style and G4Radiacode is GPL-3.0. A new MIT package converts spectra to H*(10) from a Geant4 response matrix (Casanovas 2016 method). GADRAS, the reference detector-response code, is not openly available.
- **Data:** a 2026 CC BY dataset (EURAMET 22NRM07 GuideRadPROS) gives energy- and angle-response test results for active personal and area dosimeters. No open, labelled CsI(Tl) spectra set from a small detector was found, and no curated Radiacode spectra archive.

### Inferences
- A complete open M1 forward-model chain is now possible:
  - G4Radiacode (or GATE) gives the crystal response matrix R(E, θ).
  - Gamma Dose Converter's Casanovas-style G(E) converts spectrum to H*(10).
  - A phantom MC (GATE/PHITS) gives the photon fluence spectrum at the detector position for each source organ.
  - PyRIID or becquerel generate and fit synthetic spectra.
  - SpecUtils handles file I/O.
  - InterSpec serves for manual checks.
  Licences mix BSD/MIT/LGPL (fine in a product) and GPL (G4Radiacode; keep offline).
- Spectral observables are likely to beat dose rate as M1 inputs. For example, the ratio of the 208 keV peak to the low-energy scatter continuum should change with source depth and the body-attenuation path. That argues for logging full spectra on the Radiacode. This is our inference; no source in this round tested it on patients.
- The GuideRadPROS dataset gives an independent picture of how much commercial EPDs deviate with energy and angle. That spread is a prior for how far a consumer CsI dosimeter might deviate at 113/208 keV before IRSA's own calibration.

### Gaps
- No paper was found that simulates a single CsI/GM detector worn on the collar, chest or wrist of an RPT patient, or reports angular response of a body-worn detector with the body as backscatter and attenuator.
- No independent peer-reviewed energy or angular characterisation of Radiacode devices was found.
- GADRAS availability outside US national labs is unknown.
- No open CsI(Tl) or GM response-function dataset (measured) was found; two relevant 2026 Zenodo records are restricted.

## Q4. Closing gaps from the earlier round: MC code licences, 177Lu dose-rate constants, OpenDose licence, radot on PyPI, open buildup-factor alternatives

### Takeaway
- **MC code licences.** MC-GPU is public domain. SIMIND v9.0 is a free download "exclusively for research, educational, and training purposes". FLUKA needs an approved Single User Licence; source code goes only to academic institutes holding a CERN institutional licence. MCNP6.3 is RSICC package CCC-870, and source code is export-controlled and needs a justification. penEasy (v2024-03-13) is downloadable from UPC, but its licence text was not found; PENELOPE itself is NEA-1525.
- **radot is not on PyPI** as of 2026-11-23 (HTTP 404), even though its README says `pip install radot`.
- **177Lu Γ.** Smith & Stabin give 0.181 R·cm²/(mCi·h) (via Oumano 2025, CC BY). Peplow 2020 gives effective-dose constants: 177Lu 4.92 × 10⁻⁶ (SCALE data) or 5.18 × 10⁻⁶ (ICRP 107 data) mSv·h⁻¹·MBq⁻¹ at 1 m, and 131I 5.47–5.48 × 10⁻⁵.
- **Buildup factors.** Open G-P coefficient sources remain thin. The coefficients sit in ANS-6.4.3, with refinements in JAERI-M reports (INIS records). Free calculators exist: Phy-X/PSD (registration) and Taylor-form or MC-derived buildup in MIT Python codes.

### Inferences
- Unit bridge (our calculation): 0.181 R·cm²/(mCi·h) under RG 8.39's 1 R = 10 mSv convention is **0.00489 mSv·m²/(GBq·h)**. That matches the 0.00489 value the NRC draft appears to attribute to Smith & Stabin. With 1 R ≈ 8.76 mGy air kerma it is about 0.0043 mGy·m²/(GBq·h). Peplow's effective-dose values (0.00492–0.00518) sit between these. Bellamy's theoretical 0.007636 mSv/h/GBq is about 1.5× higher.
- So the M2-NRC baseline for 177Lu depends on which Γ is chosen, across a range of about 1.8× (0.0043–0.0076). IRSA should fix one Γ, preferably Peplow ICRP 107 effective dose (5.18 µSv·h⁻¹·GBq⁻¹ at 1 m), which is consistent with ICRP 116 receptor dosimetry, and report the others as sensitivity cases.
- For 225Ac, Peplow's parent-only value (2.8 × 10⁻⁶) understates the photon output. A chain-equilibrium Γ must sum the 221Fr and 213Bi constants weighted by branching. Peplow's table can supply the components (213Bi was not extracted here).
- Given MCNP export control and FLUKA/PENELOPE registration hurdles, the IAEA-TC-portable MC stack remains GATE/Geant4 + OpenMC + PHITS (earlier notes). MC-GPU (public domain) and SIMIND (free, research-only) are optional add-ons.

## Q6. 2024–2026 AI surrogates for external dose fields or shielding in other domains, with code availability

### Takeaway
Relevant 2025–2026 work exists, but none releases code:
- **PTNO** (arXiv, Sep 2026, CC BY) trains neural operators directly on **noisy, cheap MC labels**. It shows that unbiased MC noise leaves the least-squares minimiser unchanged and that many noisy scenes beat fewer converged ones. This is directly applicable to IRSA's M3 budget.
- A **DNN organ-dose surrogate for point-source exposures** (Choi et al., NET 2026) was trained on about 54,000 Geant4 cases.
- A **CNN shielding emulator** at FRIB (Pal Chowdhury et al., NIMB 2026) predicts neutron flux and effective dose rate behind shielding.
- **Physics-informed and POD gamma-field reconstruction** from mobile detectors (Son/Tan/Zhang, arXiv 2025–2026).

No neural-field (NeRF-style) gamma-mapping work with released code was found.

### Inferences
- A defensible M3 recipe from these sources:
  1. Generate many cheap GATE runs over (biodistribution, BMI, posture, receptor position) with low statistics (PTNO argument).
  2. Train in log space on dose rate per unit activity.
  3. Add a POD/PCA baseline (Tan et al.) and the semi-analytic model as competing predictors.
  4. Calibrate with conformal methods on held-out converged runs plus measurements (Li 2024's rRMSD ~0.2 as a realism check).
- Choi et al.'s point-source → organ-dose DNN covers the receptor half of the problem. The patient half (biodistribution → photon field) can be expressed as a sum of point or line kernels, so the two can be chained, or a single surrogate trained end to end.
- Extrapolation outside the training energy range is the known weak point (XGBoost snippet; Ennassiri 2026 in earlier notes). 225Ac (218/440 keV) should get its own MC data, not be extrapolated from 177Lu/131I.

### Gaps
- None of the surrogate papers found releases code or training data.
- No neural-field / implicit-representation model of gamma dose fields with released code was found.
- No AI surrogate for patient-to-bystander external dose in nuclear medicine was found.
- Full texts of Choi et al. 2026 and Pal Chowdhury et al. 2026 were not read (publisher 403), so accuracy figures are from abstracts or search summaries.
