# Related papers IRSA should build on (2018–2026): annotated bibliography

Scope note (researcher, 2026-11-23). I first checked both IRSA reports and the earlier notes, then left out papers they already use. That covers Levart 2019 and 2025, Schütze 2026, Kurth 2018, Bailey 2026, Bellamy 2022 (PMC9667277, 230-patient 177Lu Γ), Cortez 2026, Ierace 2026, Monserrat Fuertes 2023, Turku/Kääriä 2024, Takai 2024, Tipnis 2026, Sheikhzadeh 2025, Swinson SNMMI 2026 (I confirmed it is a workflow proposal with no patient data), Graves 2023, CLEAN-225Ac, SELFIE, O'Callaghan 2022, WIDMApp/Morganti 2021, the UW vest, Brinks/van Gils, Zagni 2024, Kupitz 2025, Koller 2025, Bilgin 2024, Tsai 2025 and the Japanese RPS exam paper (PMC11526626).

Method. I searched the Europe PMC REST API with title- and abstract-restricted queries, plus web search. Every paper below was checked against its Europe PMC/PubMed record (title, authors, journal, DOI/PMID). Unless a row says otherwise, I read the abstract.

Flags used in the tables:
- **[abstract-only]**: a conference abstract with no peer-reviewed paper.
- **[title-only]**: the record exists, but I did not read an abstract or full text.
- **[snippet]**: the figure comes from a search-engine summary or news coverage.
- **"Data: not checked"**: I did not open the full text to look for a data-availability statement. No row reports deposited per-patient data or code unless it says so.

Cross-reference: two papers here (Bertschmann 2026 and Tsai 2025) are also catalogued in the sibling note `llm_rp_papers_corpora.md`.

## Q1. Individualised release and dose-to-others methods; measured vs calculated caregiver/public doses (177Lu, 131I, 223Ra, 225Ac); paediatric/pregnant contacts; travel, airports, cremation, emergencies

### Takeaway
The 2024–2026 literature has moved from "is outpatient RPT safe?" to "which patient-specific variables predict dose rate, and how should multi-cycle, travel and confinement doses be counted?" Three groups of papers give IRSA directly reusable material:
- **Pre-treatment predictors of discharge dose rate**: Shimizu 2026 (AUC 0.929); Wakabayashi 2025 (RMSE 2.2–3.5 µSv/h).
- **Calculated-vs-measured validation designs**: Han 2021 (n = 215); Lu 2025, where paediatric 177Lu predictions can be compared with TLD caregiver doses.
- **Overlooked dose terms**: Banci Buonamici 2025 (dose during confinement and on the trip home); Chipiga 2025 (public transport); Kranert 2022 (multiple cycles per calendar year).

A JNM 2026 survey of 102 centres found that post-treatment instructions vary widely between centres. That is the strongest recent argument for IRSA's personalised, rule-versioned guidance.

### Inferences
- Pre-treatment imaging predictors (washout rate, total uptake) achieve AUC about 0.93 or RMSE about 2–3.5 µSv/h for 177Lu-DOTATATE discharge dose rate. IRSA's wearable-updated Bayesian estimate should be benchmarked against these simpler baselines, and should beat them, before anyone claims added value.
- Both paediatric 177Lu (Lu 2025: 0.44 mSv calculated at 1 m vs 0.245 mSv measured) and 131I-MIBG (Willegaignon 2018: 11.9 mSv potential vs 1.88 mSv measured) show measured caregiver doses well below conservative calculations. This supports IRSA's claim that personalisation can relax restrictions without breaching constraints, but these are small single-centre series.
- Three dose terms are often left out of release calculations: dose during confinement, dose on the journey home, and multiple cycles per year. Together they can flip a "compliant" verdict (Banci Buonamici 2025; Kranert 2022; Chipiga 2025). IRSA's engine should include all three by default.
- For alpha emitters, the risk lies in internal contamination via saliva, sweat, urine and incontinence (Wanke 2021; Dahlke 2025). A dose-rate wearable adds little for 225Ac. Contamination-behaviour guidance is the useful product there.

### Gaps
- No 2018–2026 peer-reviewed study of **hotel stays** after RPT turned up. I found **airport alarm** evidence only as older case reports and guidance; the frequency for 177Lu is unknown.
- No 2018–2026 study measured **pregnant contacts' doses** directly. Evidence is limited to models (Aziz 2022; Liu 2024) and patient-pregnancy event reports.
- Cremation: only the 2019 JAMA letter, whose full text I did not read. I found no 177Lu-PSMA crematorium dosimetry series.
- I found no peer-reviewed home measurements or release data for 225Ac outpatients, only inpatient-room contamination (Dahlke 2025 abstract; CLEAN-225Ac).

## Q2. Home contamination, excretion and waste (urine/toilet, saliva, sewage, storage vs disposal, 177mLu, 225Ac; effectiveness of hygiene instructions)

### Takeaway
Recent room-surface studies agree that the **toilet bowl is the dominant contamination hotspot for 177Lu**, and that residual room contamination after 177Lu-DOTATATE is much lower than after 131I. The saliva source term for 177Lu-PSMA is about 4× that of 177Lu-DOTATOC. Sewage dose from 177Lu is negligible (about 8.7 µSv/yr modelled for Tehran). Waste storage time depends on the **production route**: 177mLu impurity keeps waste above background beyond 120 days, whereas no-carrier-added waste reaches background by about day 77. I found **no study that measured whether hygiene instructions work** in patients' homes.

## Q4. Patient experience, psychosocial burden of isolation and restrictions, preferences, compliance

### Takeaway
Quantitative studies from 2025–2026 show anxiety and sleep disturbance peaking around 131I treatment and hospitalisation:
- Anxiety: 23.6% → 40.7% → 14.0%.
- Sleep disorders: 35.8% → 52.9% → 32.6%.
- "Radiation exposure concerns" were an independent predictor of distress.
- Patients' knowledge of 131I therapy is low (9.98/24). Higher knowledge was associated with fewer anxiety and depression symptoms.

A Charité survey (n = 46) found that RLT patients want shorter stays but with a safety net:
- 54.4% rated semi-inpatient care ideal.
- Only 8.9% wanted fully outpatient care.
- Telephone access and educational material raise acceptance.

This argues for positioning IRSA as "remote supervision for early discharge", not "replace the hospital".

## Q5. AI/ML in radiation protection (occupational/public), AI for release decisions, LLMs on RP/NM questions, digital twins

### Takeaway
Five strands are relevant:
- **Release prediction.** ML for release or discharge is still small and retrospective. SVM predicted 48-h discharge after 131I with 88% accuracy (n = 1,044). A neural network flagged relatives above 1 mSv with AUC 0.957 (n = 99).
- **Occupational dose.** Monte Carlo-trained ML surrogates for staff dose are now well developed in interventional radiology: an ensemble reached about 3.7% relative error, and a 3D U-Net infers in 0.2 s. This is the same design as IRSA's surrogate.
- **LLMs on exams.** On radiation-protection exams, GPT-4 failed a health-physics mock exam (61.7% vs a 67% pass mark). 2025 reasoning models passed Japanese X-ray and radiotherapy certification exams (o1-preview 71–87%; GPT-5 Pro 94.7%). GPT-4o failed the **laws and regulations** subject.
- **LLM patient education.** RLT and PRRT studies show 7–8% medically incorrect answers, weaker performance outside English, and specific errors about **institution-dependent hospitalisation rules**. That is exactly the content IRSA's rule engine should own.
- **Governance.** ICRP Committee 3's AI working party (2026) lists occupational dose prediction as promising but early in validation.

### Inferences
- Across the exam studies, frontier models now recall RP and medical-physics facts well (85–95%). Persistent weak spots are **jurisdiction-specific law** (Goto 2024), **calculation** (Bertschmann 2026), **non-English output** (Habouzit 2026), **institution-dependent hospitalisation rules** (Habouzit 2026) and **temporal drift** (Effraimidis 2026). These match IRSA's planned split of work: rules in a versioned engine, numbers from the physics engine, the LLM for wording only.
- The Monte Carlo-to-ML surrogate pattern is established for occupational dose (Harb 2026 ×2; Hışıroğlu 2025). IRSA applies it to a patient-as-source, public-receptor problem. That is a new application rather than a new method, and papers should frame it that way.

### Gaps
- I still found no peer-reviewed study that evaluates LLM answers to **post-RPT radiation-protection instructions** (distance, toilet, travel, pregnancy) against IAEA, ICRP or national guidance. The closest are Habouzit 2026 and Speicher 2025 for PRRT, Tsai 2025 for RAI, and Bilgin 2024 for PSMA.
- I found no published AI system that makes **patient release decisions** with prospective validation. All are retrospective.
- I found no digital-twin study for radiation protection of the public.

## Q6. Health economics and capacity: inpatient isolation vs outpatient RPT

### Takeaway
I found **no published cost analysis comparing inpatient isolation with outpatient 177Lu or 131I administration per bed-day**. The economic case currently rests on capacity modelling:
- Germany: about 234,000 nuclear-medicine therapy bed-days per year with a reserve of only about 19,000 (about 63 beds).
- Japan: discrete-event simulation predicts a significant rise in patients waiting more than 180 days within 4 years of 177Lu-PSMA introduction. Beds are the main cause, and drainage capacity is an added limit.

Published cost-effectiveness analyses of 177Lu-PSMA and 177Lu-DOTATATE address the drug against alternatives, not where it is given.

### Inferences
- The strongest economic argument for IRSA in DACH and Japan is **throughput under a bed constraint** (Zippel 2021; Mizowaki 2025), not per-bed-day savings. A health-economic model for IRSA should be a capacity/queueing model, for example by extending Mizowaki's discrete-event simulation with IRSA-enabled early discharge, rather than a classic cost-effectiveness analysis.

### Gaps
- No published per-bed-day cost of shielded isolation (DRG or NUB) for 177Lu or 131I in Germany, Japan or China was found in this sweep.
- No study quantifies caregivers' lost workdays or patients' productivity losses from home restrictions after RPT.
- I did not open the full texts of the drug cost-effectiveness analyses to see whether they model inpatient days.
