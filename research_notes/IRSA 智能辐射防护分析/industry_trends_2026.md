# 2026 Industry Trends Relevant to IRSA: the RPT/Theranostics Market, Its Capacity Bottlenecks, and the Healthcare AI-Agent Industry

Research date: 2026-11-19. Scope: (a) RPT market, capacity bottlenecks and existing digital tools for RPT patients; (b) healthcare AI agents, wearables, reimbursement and business models. Unless a source is marked with another year, its data is from 2026. Many trade-press summaries could not be checked against primary documents; where that is the case, the bullet says so.

## 1. RPT market in 2025–2026: sales, indication expansions, new approvals and readouts, volume forecasts, isotope supply

### Takeaway
Novartis' Pluvicto is now a roughly USD 2.6–2.7B/yr product. That is about USD 1.97B in 9M 2026, up 41% cc. On 31 Jul 2026 the FDA approved it for PSMA+ mHSPC (PSMAddition), so it is now labelled for every stage of PSMA+ metastatic prostate cancer and the eligible pool roughly doubles. Lutathera is flat at about USD 0.87B/yr and now faces two other approved 177Lu-dotatate products. The alpha-emitter era is arriving: BMS/RayzeBio's 225Ac-dotatate (RYZ101) produced the first positive Phase 3 for an alpha RLT in Oct 2026. Several 2026 setbacks show the field is not frictionless: ITM's 177Lu-edotreotide received a CRL, and Lilly/Point's PSMA programme stalled. Lu-177 supply is diversifying. Ac-225 is still the tight isotope.

### Inferences
- Moving Pluvicto into mHSPC means more cycles per patient over a longer course of disease. Those patients are fitter and more often working or living with families. The number of post-discharge radiation-protection episodes therefore rises faster than the patient count, which helps IRSA's case. In Germany, Austria, Italy and Japan, where inpatient stays are mandatory, the same shift multiplies demand for shielded beds.
- 177Lu, with gamma emissions at 113/208 keV and GBq-level activities, is the main driver of isolation and dose-rate-based release. 225Ac agents are given at much lower administered activity. My understanding, which these sources do not verify, is that this gives much lower external dose rates and that the agents are generally given outpatient. If alpha agents take share after 2028, IRSA's dose-rate monitoring matters less, while contamination and excreta hygiene guidance matters more (see the CLEAN-225Ac contamination data in section 2). IRSA's dosimeter would need beta/alpha-contamination logic or a reframed purpose to stay relevant.
- Despite the 2026 setbacks, 177Lu-SSTR and 177Lu-PSMA supply is multiplying: generics, BEXLUTRY, ECLIPSE and ITM's planned resubmission. That points to lower drug prices and higher volume, and so to more strain on capacity downstream rather than on isotopes.

## 2. Capacity bottlenecks: shielded rooms, workforce, inpatient vs outpatient models, society and agency statements

### Takeaway
How much capacity a country has depends heavily on its radiation-protection rules. The US (NRC 5 mSv to others), France, Spain and Australia treat 177Lu-PSMA largely as an outpatient therapy. Germany, Austria, Italy (in parts) and Japan require stays in shielded therapy wards, at least 48 h per cycle in Germany. The Japanese society explicitly expects waiting lists because few facilities have radiation treatment rooms. Workforce shortages are documented across nuclear medicine physicians, physicists, technologists and radiopharmacists. EANM's 2026 materials say systems are "not equipped to meet rising demand" and that implementation of Euratom 2013/59 patient-release criteria varies widely. No 2026 dataset counts isolation beds or waiting times, so this evidence is mostly qualitative.

### Cited Findings
**Release rules and inpatient vs outpatient**
- The joint EANM/SNMMI/IAEA enabling guide says that in Germany, Austria and Italy "hospitalisation is in any case mandatory following radionuclide therapy." Where a release threshold of 30 µSv/h at 1 m applies, 177Lu-PSMA can be given as an outpatient therapy with discharge within about 6 h. Source: [EANM/SNMMI/IAEA enabling guide (EJNMMI 2022)](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00259-022-05785-x)
- **Germany.** A 2018 study reports patients must stay at least 48 h, until the dose rate at 2 m keeps public exposure under 1 mSv/yr. At 72 h discharge, the 1 mSv/yr limit at 2 m would be exceeded in 10% and 15% of patients receiving 3 and 4 cycles per year. Source: [EJNMMI Research 2018](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s13550-018-0386-4); [encyclopedia.pub summary](https://encyclopedia.pub/entry/9741)
- **Germany, 2026 practice.** Aachen University Hospital's patient information (version 04/2026) says each 177Lu-PSMA cycle requires at least 48 h inpatient under radiation-protection rules, with 4–6 cycles at about 6-week intervals. Dresden's 2025 flyer cites 3–4 days on the therapy ward. Essen runs a dedicated "FastTrack PSMA-RLT" intake address. Source: [UK Aachen patient info 04/2026 (PDF)](https://www.ukaachen.de/fileadmin/files/klinik-nuklearmedizin/Patienteninformationen/AM-DC-Patienteninfo_177Lu-PSMA_Therapie_0426.pdf); [UK Dresden flyer 2025](https://www.uniklinikum-dresden.de/de/das-klinikum/kliniken-polikliniken-institute/nuk/therapie/FlyerPRLT2025.pdf); [UK Essen brochure 2024](https://nuklearmedizin.uk-essen.de/wp-content/uploads/2024/09/2024_Broschuere_psma_grafe_TH300424_ANSICHT.pdf)
- **US, France, Spain.** A JNM 2024 article says hospitalisation is not strictly necessary in these countries, and same-day release occurs in some Italian regions. Australia allows outpatient treatment, whereas much of Europe needs 1–3 days in shielded wards. US NRC rules allow release if the total effective dose to others stays under 5 mSv. Source: [JNM 2024, "177Lu-PSMA (R)Evolution"](https://jnm.snmjournals.org/content/early/2024/08/08/jnumed.124.267951); [PMC outpatient feasibility study](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11619580/)
- **Japan.** The JSNM notice on 177Lu-PSMA-617 approval says administration must take place in a controlled area with hospitalisation in a radiation treatment room or "special-measures room." It notes this "limits the number of facilities," so waiting patients are expected. Patients must also manage daily living without nursing help during the stay. Source: [JSNM notice (Japanese), Oct 2025](https://jsnm.org/wp_jsnm/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/放射性リガンド療法「177Lu-PSMA-617」承認に際してのお知らせ.pdf)
- **EU variation.** An EANM EU Policy Symposium report (Dec 2025) found significant variation between countries in how Euratom 2013/59 is implemented for RPT, including patient-release criteria. Source: [EANM "From Policy to Patients" report](https://eanm.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/EU_Policy_Symposium1_Report.pdf) (via search summary)
- **EANM 2026 narrative.** The 2026 EU-affairs document "Nuclear Medicine: What it is. Where it goes. What it needs." cites fragmented legislation and training barriers and calls for EU workforce planning. It also asks that key radioisotopes be brought under the Critical Medicines Act and supports the European Radioisotopes Valley Initiative (ERVI). Search summaries of EANM materials say "current healthcare systems [are] not equipped to meet rising demand, leading to delays and inequitable patient access." Source: [EANM EU Affairs Report 2026 (PDF)](https://eanm.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/EU_Affairs_Report_OverarchingNarrative_2026.pdf)
- **EANM 2026 congress (Barcelona, 17–21 Oct).** The programme included a session titled "Radiation Protection in Patients Undergoing Radioligand Therapy" (21 Oct) and a session on RLT regulation, readiness and patient access. I found no published outcomes. Source: [EANM'26 Final Programme](https://eanm26.eanm.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/EANM26_Final_Programme.pdf). The ECMP 2026 course (Valencia) covers patient release after 131I/177Lu under IAEA GSR Part 3 and BSSD 2013/59/Euratom ([EFOMP ECMP 2026 course PDF](https://ecmp2026.efomp.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/2609-6TH-ECMP-VALENCIA-COURSES_Nuclear-Medicine_v05.pdf)).
- **225Ac contamination (CLEAN-225Ac, published Sept 2026).** Post-therapy contamination was found in 6.5% of wipe samples and in 62.5% of therapy cycles. The authors recommend targeted hygiene and radiation-protection measures, especially for incontinent patients and caregivers. Source: [ScienceDirect, EANM Innovation](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S3051291326002600)

**Physical capacity**
- A recruiter-industry article (Dec 2025) claims "most hospitals have only two or three" lead-shielded RLT rooms. It describes "Ghost Clinics," theranostic centres running below capacity because staff are missing. It cites no data. Source: [ProGen Search](https://progensearch.com/radiopharma-clinical-network-bottleneck/) (low evidentiary value)
- **UK.** RCR (2021) found molecular radiotherapy provision "not uniform," with demand set to "increase dramatically" and "most UK regions and nations… not prepared." An HSJ roundtable said many nuclear medicine services are "nearing or exceeding capacity" and that lead-lined rooms are needed for 131I/177Lu. NHS England is assessing service readiness under a new service specification. Novartis-funded "collaborative working" projects at Barts and Southampton aim to cut MRT waiting lists. Source: [RCR review](https://www.rcr.ac.uk/our-services/all-our-publications/clinical-oncology-publications/review-of-molecular-radiotherapy-services-in-the-uk/); [HSJ roundtable](https://www.hsj.co.uk/service-redesign/roundtable-understanding-challenges-in-nuclear-medicine-delivery/7035160.article); [Parliamentary answer HL479](https://parallelparliament.co.uk/question/HL479/radiotherapy); [Novartis–Barts project](https://www.novartis.com/uk-en/about/partnerships/collaborative-working/barts-health-nhs-trust-st-bartholomews-hospital); [Novartis–Southampton project](https://prod1.novartis.com/uk-en/about/partnerships/collaborative-working/southampton-general-hospital)
- **IAEA / LMIC.** An IAEA-perspective review states that in LMICs one SPECT scanner may serve 33 million people versus 57,000 in high-income countries, and one PET unit 250 million in LICs versus 286,000 in HICs. It notes 177Lu shortages and single-supplier dependence. Source: [PMC12602077, "Global Challenges in the Provision of Theranostic Services – IAEA Perspective"](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12602077/). A Feb 2026 IOMP presentation documents large access disparities and calls for fast-track pathways. Source: [IOMP, Karmaker, Feb 2026](https://www.iomp.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/5-Karmaker-HTA-of-Radiotheranostics-in-Developing-Countries.pdf)

**Workforce**
- The SNMMI position paper (JNM 2025) says a shortage of professionals trained in emerging therapies limits patient access to RPT. Source: [JNM 2025 via NYSED copy](https://www.op.nysed.gov/sites/op/files/2026-09/Need%20for%20RPT%20Expertise%20-%20JNM2025.pdf). The Lancet Oncology Commission found gaps in NM physicians, physicists, technologists, radiochemists and radiopharmacists, and SNMMI's president links these to cuts in residency training. Source: [AuntMinnie "Rise of Theranostics" pt 6](https://www.auntminnie.com/clinical-news/molecular-imaging/article/15752966/the-rise-of-theranostics-part-6-private-practices-pivot-to-clinical-trials); [JNM 2023 editorial (Graham)](https://jnm.snmjournals.org/content/64/9/1356)
- Coverage of SNMMI 2026 says isotope production, radiochemistry and trained staff are not keeping pace with demand and capital. Source: [RT Medical Systems, SNMMI 2026 recap](https://rtmedical.com.br/en/snmmi-2026-theranostics-cardiac-pet/) (secondary)
- **German cross-sector care (qualitative study).** Barriers include coordination between urology, oncology and nuclear medicine. Source: [PMC12490126](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12490126/)

### Inferences
- IRSA's core claim, that patient-specific dose-rate data can safely shorten 48–72 h stays, fits best where release is set by a fixed time or a conservative dose-rate threshold and shielded beds are scarce: DACH, Italy, Japan and parts of Asia. In the US/AU outpatient model, the value shifts from freeing beds to documenting compliance, reassuring caregivers and supporting authorised-user workload.
- Under Euratom, each country implements release criteria differently, so IRSA's regulatory route is country by country. Its "optional regulator reporting" feature could help persuade national competent authorities, for example via BfS/StrlSchV in Germany, to accept individualised release.
- Workforce shortage is a second-order argument for IRSA: fewer physicist and nurse hours spent on discharge counselling and phone follow-ups. It also raises the bar, because overstretched departments will adopt only tools that need minimal integration effort.
- The CLEAN-225Ac contamination data points to a gap that a gamma dose-rate dosimeter cannot close: alpha contamination via excreta and incontinence. IRSA's LLM guidance on hygiene behaviour may matter more than its dosimetry as alpha agents grow.

### Gaps
- I found no 2025–2026 quantitative data on shielded therapy bed counts, occupancy or RLT waiting times for Germany, the UK, Japan or China.
- No 2026 SNMMI, NHS England or IAEA statement focused specifically on RPT capacity was found. EANM'26 session outcomes on radiation protection are not published.
- No current data on the share of US Pluvicto administrations done outpatient or in community settings.
- China's 2026 release rules for 177Lu (Pluvicto mHSPC approved by NMPA in Q3 2026) and capacity there are not researched.

## 3. Existing digital tools for RPT patients: apps, chatbots, remote monitoring, patient dosimeters, competitors

### Takeaway
No commercial "radiation-safety companion" combining a patient-worn dosimeter with conversational guidance after RPT turned up. What exists is (i) printed or PDF discharge instructions, (ii) a few 131I patient apps focused on education and diet, (iii) research-stage wearables, mainly for organ dosimetry rather than public protection, (iv) Bluetooth staff dosimeters (Mirion, Thermo Fisher) not marketed to patients, (v) clinician-facing theranostics chatbots in research, and (vi) incumbent workflow and dosimetry software (GE HealthCare, DOSIsoft). A patent describing a patient-radiation dosimeter linked to a smartphone shows the concept is not new in principle, which matters for IRSA's patent novelty.

### Cited Findings
- **Patient wearables (research).** At SNMMI 2021 a wearable vest was presented to track 177Lu-DOTATATE organ washout at home, aiming for kidney washout accuracy within 5%. A later abstract found a sparse vest could monitor organs and tumours but still needed one 24 h SPECT/CT. Source: [AuntMinnie (2021)](https://www.auntminnie.com/clinical-news/molecular-imaging/nuclear-medicine/article/15628641/patient-vest-monitors-dose-after-radionuclide-therapy); [JNM abstract](https://jnm.snmjournals.org/content/60/supplement_1/313.abstract)
- **131I.** The CoTI collar detector measured thyroid uptake in 81 patients at 2/24 h and 8 patients up to 96 h. Ceiling-mounted remote dose-rate meters in isolation rooms reliably determine effective half-life. Source: [Sensors 2023 review](https://doi.org/10.3390/s23052599); [PMC11604915](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11604915/). An in-vitro iodine radionuclide dosimeter (IRD) for continuous patient monitoring was evaluated in 2020 ([ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/338870606_In_vitro_evaluation_of_an_iodine_radionuclide_dosimeter_IRD_for_continuous_patient_monitoring)).
- **Family exposure after 177Lu-DOTATATE.** Family members wore TLD badges for 4 weeks to test whether existing precautions are adequate. Source: [EJNMMI Physics 2025](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s40658-025-00776-x)
- **Concept patent.** A USPTO patent (US 9,972,189) describes a personal radiation dosimeter and alert system tracking radiation from a person treated with nuclear medicine, paired with a smartphone or smartwatch via Bluetooth or WiFi. Source: [USPTO PDF](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/9972189)
- **Staff dosimeters.** Mirion Instadose2 (BLE, app readout, beta-sensitive, nuclear medicine use) and Thermo Fisher NetDose Pro (Bluetooth, mobile app) are commercial products aimed at occupational users, not patients. Source: [Radiology Business](https://radiologybusiness.com/topics/patient-care/new-digital-dosimeters-give-real-time-radiation-dose-reads); [PSA Inc.](https://www.psa.inc/company/news/mobile-solutions-in-radiation-dosimetry)
- **Chatbots.** A 2025 *Theranostics* paper built a context-augmented (RAG) LLM chatbot for health professionals on 35 PRRT questions and flagged hallucination as a radiation-protection risk. Source: [Theranostics 2025;15:5693](https://www.thno.org/v15p5693.htm). A 2021 IBM-Watson chatbot was built for radiotherapy staff radiation-safety training ([IOP SciNotes](https://iopscience.iop.org/article/10.1088/2633-1357/ac1f88)). Mass General Brigham is testing an LLM chatbot based on ChatGPT 5.2 in a secure Azure environment, with physician-reviewed sources and safety guardrails, for prostate external-beam RT patient education. Source: [ClinicalTrials.gov NCT07719062](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT07719062). This is the closest analogue to IRSA's chatbot layer, but it is not RPT-specific.
- **131I patient apps.** The Thai "Love Thyroid" app delivers infographics and video. The DietLens app reduced anxiety around the low-iodine diet. A 2023 physician-facing app generates patient-specific printable post-131I instructions, replacing error-prone handwritten ones. Source: [Ramathibodi journal](https://he02.tci-thaijo.org/index.php/ramajournal/article/download/258474/176997); [JMIR Formative 2025](https://formative.jmir.org/2025/1/e60588/); [JNM abstract](https://jnm.snmjournals.org/node/112772)
- **Incumbent software.** GE HealthCare's Theranostics Pathway Manager (a Command Center tile) tracks patient readiness across labs, scheduling and orders. DOSIsoft spun out "DOSIsoft Theranostics" (26 May 2026) around its PLANET dosimetry platform for cycle-by-cycle monitoring. Source: [GE HealthCare IR](https://investor.gehealthcare.com/news-releases/news-release-details/ge-healthcare-drives-innovation-theranostics-latest); [DOSIsoft, 26 May 2026](https://www.dosisoft.com/2026/05/26/dosisoft-launches-dosisoft-theranostics-dedicated-entity-accelerate-software-innovation-personalized-nuclear-medicine-theranostics/)
- **Theranostics education.** SNMMI technologist-education literature stresses competence in "post-therapy monitoring" and unsealed-source safety, which shows the counselling burden falls on staff. Source: [JNMT Supplement](https://tech.snmjournals.org/content/53/Supplement_1/49S)

### Inferences
- The white space IRSA targets, patient-side real-time dose plus contextual LLM guidance plus a regulator-grade log, appears genuinely unoccupied commercially as of Nov 2026. The building blocks are commoditised, though: BLE dosimeters, RAG chatbots and dosimetry software. The defensible moat is therefore regulatory acceptance and clinical validation, not hardware.
- Prior art such as US 9,972,189 and the wearable vest work may narrow IRSA's patent claims. Novelty will probably have to rest on the specific mix of an MC-trained surrogate dose model, occupancy/map context and neuro-symbolic guardrails.
- Likely competitive threats: (a) Novartis, Curium or Lantheus bundling a branded companion app into patient-support programmes; (b) dosimetry vendors such as Mirion and Thermo extending staff badges to patients; (c) GE HealthCare or Siemens extending theranostics workflow software to the post-discharge phase.

### Gaps
- No startup explicitly marketing a "radiation safety companion" or theranostics patient-engagement app was found. This may reflect search limits; a funding-database search (Crunchbase/PitchBook) was not possible.
- Whether Novartis' Pluvicto/Lutathera patient-support programmes include digital radiation-safety tools was not verified.
- No hospital programme using patient-worn dosimeters routinely after 177Lu/131I discharge was found. The evidence is all small research studies.

### Inferences
- The new 2–15-day RPM code (99445) fits the post-RPT period well. 177Lu dose rates fall below typical public thresholds within days to about 2 weeks. RPM, however, requires an FDA-defined medical device collecting **physiologic** data. Whether a patient-emitted dose-rate measurement counts as "physiologic" is doubtful, so IRSA may need to pair the dosimeter with a recognised physiologic signal, or seek a separate code or bundled payment. This is my interpretation; I have not checked it against a CMS ruling.
- Under the Jan 2026 FDA guidance, IRSA's patient-facing, directive, time-sensitive radiation guidance (for example "keep 2 m from your grandchild for the next 18 h") would probably not fit the general-wellness or non-device CDS carve-outs. In the US it would most likely be SaMD. In the EU it would most likely be MDR Class IIa or higher plus a high-risk system under the AI Act (inference).
- Big-tech health agents already ingest Apple Health and Health Connect streams. If IRSA's dosimeter wrote dose-rate data into HealthKit or Health Connect, it could feed those ecosystems. That is a distribution option, and also a sign that big platforms could add the feature cheaply.

- No 2026 data on Apple Watch hypertension, Samsung or Oura medical clearances, or on-device LLM health features was retrieved.
- No 2026 Europe or Asia equivalents of RPM reimbursement were researched, for example Germany's DiGA/DiPA or Japan's remote-monitoring fees.

## 6. Business model and adoption risks: who pays, evidence needed, pharma-sponsored digital companions

### Takeaway
The plausible payers for IRSA are, in order: (1) **pharma sponsors** (Novartis, Curium/Lantheus, BMS, ITM), who have strong incentives to remove capacity bottlenecks that cap RLT volume and already fund capacity projects; (2) **hospitals and treatment centres**, where freed shielded-bed days or faster turnover pay for the tool, mostly in DACH, Italy and Japan; (3) **payers and RPM codes** (US), which are limited. Pharma-sponsored oncology companions are common: Merck KGaA/Huma, AZ/Daiichi with Mika, Sidekick. Under FDA's PDURS framework, though, software claims need evidence of added clinical benefit, or proof the software is essential for safe use, before they can go on the label. Adoption will depend on prospective evidence that IRSA keeps third-party doses within limits while shortening stays, and on regulator acceptance.

### Inferences
- **Most likely early business model:** a pharma-sponsored or co-developed RPT "safety companion" bundled into a patient-support programme. It would first be positioned as compliance and education support rather than as a label claim, to avoid the PDURS evidence bar. A label-relevant claim, such as "enables outpatient administration in jurisdiction X," would need a prospective study. The ideal sponsors are those whose volume is capped by isolation beds in Europe and Japan: Novartis, and Curium after the Lantheus deal.
- **Hospital ROI** is strongest where mandatory stays exist. Converting a 48 h stay to same-day or 24 h discharge frees shielded-bed capacity, which is the binding constraint, so it directly raises the number of cycles a centre can deliver. US hospitals already treat outpatient, so their ROI comes from staff time, compliance and caregiver reassurance.
- **Evidence ladder:** (1) phantom and Monte Carlo validation of the surrogate dose model; (2) a prospective cohort comparing IRSA-guided behaviour with measured caregiver TLD doses, extending the design of the EJNMMI Physics 2025 family-exposure study; (3) regulator-endorsed pilots of individualised release, for example under the IAEA TC project; (4) health-economic modelling of freed bed-days. LLM-safety evaluation, with RPT-specific rubrics and multi-turn agent tests, will be expected alongside these, given 2026 scrutiny of consumer health chatbots.
- **Key risks:** a SaMD/MDR plus AI Act regulatory burden; liability for erroneous guidance leading to third-party overexposure; regulators reluctant to move from fixed rules to individualised release; a shift toward alpha emitters that weakens the dose-rate value; and big-tech health agents or pharma apps offering "good enough" generic guidance.
