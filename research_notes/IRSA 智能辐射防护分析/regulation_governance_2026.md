## 1. EU AI Act in 2026: Digital Omnibus, high-risk timing for AI medical devices, GPAI, MDCG guidance and MDR Rule 11

### Takeaway
The AI Omnibus is now law: Regulation (EU) 2026/1744, OJ 24 Jul 2026, in force 27 Jul 2026. High-risk obligations for AI that is also an MDR device needing a notified body move from 2 Aug 2027 to **2 Aug 2028**. MDR/IVDR **stay in Annex I Section A**, so AI medical devices are still "high-risk AI". The Commission's Dec 2025 proposal to move them to Section B is still being negotiated. Chatbot transparency (Art. 50) has applied since **2 Aug 2026**. GPAI obligations have applied since 2 Aug 2025. A patient-facing LLM that gives radiation-protection behaviour advice after therapy will very likely be at least MDR Class IIa under Rule 11 if it qualifies as a medical device. That would make it high-risk under AI Act Art. 6(1).

### Cited Findings
**Digital Omnibus on AI: legislative status (current)**
- Regulation (EU) 2026/1744 was adopted 8 Jul 2026, published in the OJ 24 Jul 2026, and "entered into force on 27 July 2026" (per its Art. 4). Brief dated 3 Aug 2026. — [QLE Regulatory Brief](https://www.qle.be/the-qle-regulatory-brief/ai-acts-digital-omnibus-is-now-law-ai-medical-devices-get-until-2-august-2028-but-notified-bodies-face-a-28-january-2028-deadline)
- Earlier state: provisional political agreement on 6 May 2026, confirmed by the Council 13 May (an earlier trilogue failed on 28 Apr). On 27 May 2026 formal adoption and OJ publication were "expected in the coming weeks". — [Gibson Dunn, 27 May 2026](https://www.gibsondunn.com/eu-ai-act-omnibus-agreement-postponed-high-risk-deadlines-and-other-key-changes/)
- Date conflicts: other sources give the provisional deal as 7 May 2026, with EP endorsement 16 Jun and Council adoption 29 Jun 2026. QLE says adoption was 8 Jul. All agree on OJ 24 Jul and entry into force 27 Jul. — [Usercentrics](https://usercentrics.com/knowledge-hub/eu-ai-act-high-risk-delay-article-50-transparency-consent/); [Produktkanzlei, 21 May 2026](https://www.produktkanzlei.com/en/2026/05/21/digital-omnibus-medical-devices-to-remain-under-dual-ai-regulation/). The EUR-Lex text should be checked for the exact adoption date.
- New application dates: Annex III stand-alone high-risk AI moves to **2 Dec 2027**. Annex I product-embedded AI (medical devices included) moves to **2 Aug 2028**. The fixed dates replace the Commission's originally proposed trigger tied to standards availability. — [Gibson Dunn](https://www.gibsondunn.com/eu-ai-act-omnibus-agreement-postponed-high-risk-deadlines-and-other-key-changes/); [QLE](https://www.qle.be/the-qle-regulatory-brief/ai-acts-digital-omnibus-is-now-law-ai-medical-devices-get-until-2-august-2028-but-notified-bodies-face-a-28-january-2028-deadline) ("fixed, not conditional on standards being ready")
- MDR/IVDR remain in Section A. QLE: "Medical devices were not moved out of Annex I Section A." The Omnibus moved the Machinery Regulation to Section B, not MDR/IVDR. — [QLE](https://www.qle.be/the-qle-regulatory-brief/ai-acts-digital-omnibus-is-now-law-ai-medical-devices-get-until-2-august-2028-but-notified-bodies-face-a-28-january-2028-deadline); [Produktkanzlei](https://www.produktkanzlei.com/en/2026/05/21/digital-omnibus-medical-devices-to-remain-under-dual-ai-regulation/) ("Medical Devices to remain under dual AI regulation")
- Notified bodies already designated under MDR/IVDR "must apply for AI Act designation by 28 January 2028" if they will assess AI Act conformity. Scope uses new Annex XIV codes. — [QLE](https://www.qle.be/the-qle-regulatory-brief/ai-acts-digital-omnibus-is-now-law-ai-medical-devices-get-until-2-august-2028-but-notified-bodies-face-a-28-january-2028-deadline)
- Delegated acts under the new Art. 2(13), defining where AI Act requirements may be limited because sectoral law gives "equivalent protection", are due by 2 Aug 2027. No content proposed yet. National AI sandboxes are due by 2 Aug 2027. — [QLE](https://www.qle.be/the-qle-regulatory-brief/ai-acts-digital-omnibus-is-now-law-ai-medical-devices-get-until-2-august-2028-but-notified-bodies-face-a-28-january-2028-deadline)
- Grandfathering (amended Art. 111(2)): high-risk systems placed on the market before the relevant date are generally outside the requirements unless they undergo significant design changes. — [QLE](https://www.qle.be/the-qle-regulatory-brief/ai-acts-digital-omnibus-is-now-law-ai-medical-devices-get-until-2-august-2028-but-notified-bodies-face-a-28-january-2028-deadline)
- Art. 50 transparency (telling people they are interacting with AI) is enforceable from **2 Aug 2026**. Only Art. 50(2) watermarking for systems already on the market gets a grace period, to 2 Dec 2026. — [Gibson Dunn](https://www.gibsondunn.com/eu-ai-act-omnibus-agreement-postponed-high-risk-deadlines-and-other-key-changes/); [QLE](https://www.qle.be/the-qle-regulatory-brief/ai-acts-digital-omnibus-is-now-law-ai-medical-devices-get-until-2-august-2028-but-notified-bodies-face-a-28-january-2028-deadline)
- GPAI obligations are unchanged and have applied since 2 Aug 2025. AI Office competence and enforcement powers are clarified and reinforced. AI literacy (Art. 4, applicable since 2 Feb 2025) is softened to "support" literacy "rather than to guarantee a specific level". — [Gibson Dunn](https://www.gibsondunn.com/eu-ai-act-omnibus-agreement-postponed-high-risk-deadlines-and-other-key-changes/)
- Commentators call this a deferral, not a repeal. No obligations removed. — [CSA research note](https://labs.cloudsecurityalliance.org/research/csa-research-note-eu-ai-act-high-risk-deadline-omnibus-20260/); [Usercentrics](https://usercentrics.com/knowledge-hub/eu-ai-act-high-risk-delay-article-50-transparency-consent/)

**Pending MDR/IVDR targeted revision (COM(2025) 1023, 16 Dec 2025): earlier proposal, not law**
- It would move MDR/IVDR to AI Act Annex I Section B, so AI Act high-risk requirements would largely stop applying directly. The Commission would set AI-specific requirements through MDR delegated or implementing acts that "take into account" AI Act requirements. Notified bodies assessing AI devices would need to meet AI Act Art. 31. MDR competent authorities would have to cooperate with AI Act market-surveillance authorities. The revision also adds cybersecurity GSPRs and new reporting of exploited vulnerabilities (MDR Art. 87a), and adds regulatory sandboxes. — [Osborne Clarke, 28 Jan 2026](https://www.osborneclarke.com/insights/eu-mdr-and-ivdr-poised-remain-main-framework-medical-ai-draft-ai-interface-reforms); [Gleiss Lutz](https://www.gleisslutz.com/en/know-how/radical-simplification-mdr-and-ivdr-and-broad-inapplicability-ai-act-medical-devices-and-ivds); [Heuking, 22 Dec 2025](https://www.heuking.de/en/news-events/newsletter-articles/detail/revision-of-the-mdr-and-ivdr-european-commission-presents-reform-proposal-for-simplification.html)
- As of Aug 2026 this file "remains in negotiation". The Omnibus co-legislators specifically refused to make the Section B move in the AI Omnibus. — [QLE](https://www.qle.be/the-qle-regulatory-brief/ai-acts-digital-omnibus-is-now-law-ai-medical-devices-get-until-2-august-2028-but-notified-bodies-face-a-28-january-2028-deadline); [Produktkanzlei](https://www.produktkanzlei.com/en/2026/05/21/digital-omnibus-medical-devices-to-remain-under-dual-ai-regulation/)
- The MDR revision also proposes a revised Rule 11, and COCIR says several key terms are contested. — [COCIR position paper](https://www.cocir.org/wp-content/uploads/COCIR-Position-Paper-EU-MDR-Targeted-Revision-Classification-Rule-11_FINAL-.pdf)

**MDCG 2025-6 / AIB 2025-1 (FAQ on MDR/IVDR and AI Act interplay, June 2025)**
- A medical-device AI (MDAI) is high-risk under AI Act Art. 6(1) if (i) the AI is a safety component of the device or is the device itself, **and** (ii) the device needs notified-body conformity assessment under MDR/IVDR. — [OpenRegulatory explainer](https://openregulatory.com/mdcg/mdcg-2025-6); [NSF](https://www.nsf.org/za/en/life-science-regulatory-news/mdcg-2025-6-eu-commission-publishes-faq-on-interplay-between-mdr-ivdr-and-ai-act)
- Class I (self-certified) devices are not high-risk MDAI. The AI Act does not raise the MDR class. — [Dorda / search summary of MDCG 2025-6](https://dorda.at/en/news/regulatory-boundaries-age-health-ai); [OpenRegulatory](https://openregulatory.com/mdcg/mdcg-2025-6)
- Manufacturers may build AI Act testing, monitoring and documentation into existing MDR QMS, technical documentation and PMS. An MDR "user" is not automatically an AI Act "deployer". A clinical investigation under MDR can count as AI Act Art. 60 real-world testing. — [OpenRegulatory](https://openregulatory.com/mdcg/mdcg-2025-6); [Reuschlaw](https://www.reuschlaw.de/en/news/interplay-between-mdr-ivdr-and-aia-in-ai-assisted-medical-devices-mdai/)
- Earlier state: MDCG 2025-6 cited 2 Aug 2027 as the high-risk application date. The Omnibus has since replaced this with 2 Aug 2028. — [NSF](https://www.nsf.org/za/en/life-science-regulatory-news/mdcg-2025-6-eu-commission-publishes-faq-on-interplay-between-mdr-ivdr-and-ai-act); [QLE](https://www.qle.be/the-qle-regulatory-brief/ai-acts-digital-omnibus-is-now-law-ai-medical-devices-get-until-2-august-2028-but-notified-bodies-face-a-28-january-2028-deadline)

**MDR qualification and Rule 11 for an LLM patient-facing SaMD**
- MDR Annex VIII Rule 11 (regulation text): software that provides information used to take decisions with diagnostic or therapeutic purposes is **Class IIa**. It is IIb if decisions could cause serious deterioration of health or surgical intervention, and III if they could cause death or irreversible deterioration. Software monitoring physiological processes is IIa (IIb for vital parameters with immediate danger). All other software is Class I. — [MDR 2017/745 on EUR-Lex](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2017/745/oj) (text recalled from the regulation; not re-fetched this session)
- MDCG 2019-11 Rev.1 (June 2025) is the current software qualification and classification guidance. Classification depends on severity of potential clinical impact as well as function. — [GMP Insiders](https://gmpinsiders.com/mdcg-2019-11-rev1-mdr-ivdr/); [mdrregulator.com](https://mdrregulator.com/news/revised-mdcg-2019-11-guidance-updated-approach-to-qualification-and-classification-of-medical-software)
- Law-firm commentary: if a system in practice influences diagnostic or therapeutic decisions it is MDR-relevant "even if the provider denies such purpose". Generative, non-deterministic LLM chatbots are "the greatest regulatory stress point". — [Dorda](https://dorda.at/en/news/regulatory-boundaries-age-health-ai)
- No source found that assigns patient-facing LLM chatbots to a specific MDR class. Classification is case by case. — [search result set, incl. RegDesk](https://www.regdesk.co/blog/qualification-and-classification-of-software-under-eu-mdr-2017-745-and-ivdr-2017-746/)

### Inferences
- **IRSA in the EU in 2026: likely dual regulation.** The surrogate dose engine estimates patient retention or dose from wearable data. That is monitoring linked to the therapy, and possibly to dosimetry for later cycles, so it has a medical purpose under MDR Art. 2(1). The chatbot's tailored "how close / how long" advice drives behaviour after a therapeutic procedure. Under Rule 11 this is at least Class IIa. A notified body is therefore required, which makes IRSA **high-risk AI under Art. 6(1)**, with AI Act Chapter III obligations from **2 Aug 2028**. Those obligations are risk management, data governance, logging, transparency to deployers, human oversight, accuracy, robustness and cybersecurity, folded into the MDR technical file. Wrong advice could cause serious harm to third parties, such as pregnant women or children. A notified body could argue IIb, but Rule 11 speaks of harm to "a person", and the third-party risk logic is untested. That is an open question.
- A purely "general radiation-protection information" chatbot without patient-specific dose estimates might be argued to fall outside MDR. That argument is weak: IRSA's value comes from personalisation, and MDCG treats functionality in practice as decisive.
- **Art. 50 already binds IRSA if it were deployed in the EU today**: it must tell patients they are talking to an AI.
- **GPAI layer:** if IRSA builds on a third-party foundation model, the model provider carries Art. 53/55 GPAI duties (applied since Aug 2025). The IRSA manufacturer remains the MDR manufacturer and AI Act high-risk provider for the whole system. Background, not verified this session: Commission GPAI guidelines (Jul 2025) treat a downstream modifier as a new GPAI provider only above a compute threshold (about one-third of original training compute). This should be checked.
- The "regulatory reporting / non-compliance detection" module is probably not an Annex III high-risk use. Radiation regulators enforcing administrative rules are arguably not "law enforcement" in the AI Act sense, which covers criminal offences. It would still raise proportionality issues under GDPR and fundamental rights (see Q2 and Q7). Not verified with a source.
- The timeline gives IRSA a window. A concept-stage product (Nov 2026) is unlikely to reach CE marking before 2 Aug 2028, so it should plan for full AI Act high-risk compliance at launch. Grandfathering would not help it.

### Gaps
- EUR-Lex text of Regulation (EU) 2026/1744 not fetched directly. Adoption-date discrepancy (29 Jun vs 8 Jul 2026) unresolved.
- Current trilogue status of COM(2025) 1023 (MDR revision, including the Section B move and Rule 11 rewrite) after Aug 2026 not found.
- Official MDCG 2025-6 PDF not fetched. Points rely on explainers.
- No 2026 MDCG or notified-body guidance specific to generative-AI/LLM devices found.
- Whether the AI Office has issued 2026 GPAI enforcement actions or updated GPAI Code of Practice signatories was not researched.

## 2. EU data and health-data law: GDPR implications of location and activity reporting, and the EHDS timeline

### Takeaway
IRSA's location and activity telemetry, linked to a patient's therapy, is special-category health data under GDPR. Sending it to regulators for "non-compliance detection" would need a specific legal basis in Union or Member State law, a DPIA, and strict proportionality. Without that, the module is the hardest part of IRSA to deploy in the EU. EHDS (Reg. (EU) 2025/327) applies generally from 26 Mar 2027, with phased EHR-system obligations in 2029, 2031 and 2035. It matters if IRSA claims EHR interoperability.

### Cited Findings
**GDPR (Regulation (EU) 2016/679; text recalled, not re-fetched this session)** — [EUR-Lex GDPR](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/679/oj)
- Location data is personal data (Art. 4(1)). Data about a patient's radionuclide therapy and retained radioactivity is "data concerning health" (Art. 4(15)). Processing special categories is prohibited unless an Art. 9(2) exception applies, for example (h) healthcare provision under law or contract with a professional, or (i) public interest in public health on the basis of Union or Member State law with safeguards.
- Processing by or for a public authority (reporting to a radiation regulator) needs an Art. 6(1)(c) legal obligation or (e) public task, both grounded in Union or Member State law (Art. 6(3)). Consent is a weak basis where there is a power imbalance with the authority (Recital 43).
- A DPIA is mandatory for large-scale processing of special-category data and for systematic monitoring (Art. 35(3)(b)/(c)).
- If automated "non-compliance" flags have legal or similarly significant effects, such as triggering enforcement or re-hospitalisation, Art. 22 restrictions on solely automated decisions apply.

**EHDS — Regulation (EU) 2025/327**
- In force March 2025 (sources say 25 or 26 Mar 2025). General application **26 Mar 2027**. Partial application steps 26 Mar 2029, 2031 and 2035 (Art. 105). Key implementing acts due by 26 Mar 2027. — [EU Monitor](https://www.eumonitor.eu/9353000/1/j9vvik7m1c3gyxp/vmlg5gptv7zp); [Heuking](https://www.heuking.de/en/news-events/newsletter-articles/detail/eu-regulation-on-the-european-health-data-space-ehds-published.html)
- EHR systems must comply with the European EHR exchange format. Vendor-oriented sources place the first priority data categories (patient summaries, ePrescriptions) at 2029 and images, labs and discharge reports at 2031. These dates are secondary and the sources themselves advise checking the text. — [EU Monitor](https://www.eumonitor.eu/9353000/1/j9vvik7m1c3gyxp/vmlg5gptv7zp); [Pubrica timeline](https://pubrica.com/insights/eu-european-health-data-space-regulation-2025-327-timeline/); [A&O Shearman](https://www.aoshearman.com/insights/ao-shearman-on-life-sciences/european-health-data-space-regulation-sets-requirements-for-electronic-health-record-systems)
- Wellness apps fall under EHDS only if they claim interoperability with EHR systems. They then need an Art. 47 label, registration in the Art. 49 database, and user-consented sharing. — [Johner Institute](https://blog.johner-institute.com/regulatory-affairs/ehds-european-health-data-space/); [ecorpit](https://ecorpit.com/eu-ehds-implementing-acts-ehr-vendors-2027/)

### Inferences
- **Location reporting to regulators is the highest-friction element of IRSA in the EU.** No Member State radiation-protection law found requires or authorises continuous location telemetry of discharged patients. Voluntary consent to a regulator is legally fragile. A privacy-preserving design would keep raw location on the device, compute compliance locally, and share only aggregate or anonymised statistics, or summary dose estimates, with the hospital (the licensee or undertaking) rather than the regulator. That design would sit far more easily with GDPR data minimisation (Art. 5(1)(c)) and Art. 25 privacy by design.
- Describing the function as "non-compliance detection" signals enforcement surveillance. That raises Art. 8 ECHR/CFR proportionality questions and could undermine patient trust and uptake. This is an inference, consistent with WHO ethics guidance (Q7).
- If IRSA writes to hospital EHRs (dose records, discharge documentation), EHDS EHR-system requirements and the EEHRxF will apply on the 2029/2031 schedule.

### Gaps
- No EDPB guidance specific to wearable health telemetry shared with authorities was researched in this session.
- Whether any Member State (for example, Germany via StrlSchV) has a legal basis for post-discharge monitoring data flows to authorities was not found.
- EHDS article-level dates for EHR-system and medical-device interoperability claims not verified against the OJ.

## 3. US FDA: AI-enabled device software in 2025–2026

### Takeaway
As of Nov 2026, FDA's AI-enabled device software functions lifecycle guidance (draft Jan 2025) is still **draft** per available sources. The PCCP guidance for AI is final. On 6 Jan 2026 FDA issued revised final CDS and General Wellness guidances, which do not address generative AI. FDA's main 2026 generative-AI action is a non-binding **discussion paper (18 Aug 2026, docket FDA-2026-N-7874)** proposing a two-axis risk framework under which patient-facing outputs rank higher. There is still no established LLM device pathway. The only claimed "first LLM clearance" (UpDoc, K253281) is disputed: FDA's public record describes no foundation model.

### Cited Findings
- **Lifecycle guidance** ("Artificial Intelligence-Enabled Device Software Functions: Lifecycle Management and Marketing Submission Recommendations", draft Jan 2025): secondary sources report it was still draft as of mid-to-late 2026. It was on CDRH's FY2026 A-list but not issued in final form by early Aug 2026. One source says FY2027 lists it again. — [search synthesis incl. Jama Software](https://www.jamasoftware.com/blog/navigating-fda-ai-guidance-for-medical-devices-a-practical-guide/), [aimocs](https://aimocs.com/guides/fda-tplc-draft-guidance-explained), [Quality Smart Solutions](https://qualitysmartsolutions.com/news/fdas-2026-ai-medical-device-guidance-signals-new-expectations-for-manufacturers/) (secondary; FDA guidance database not checked directly)
- **PCCP guidance** for AI-enabled device software functions is final (issued Dec 2024). One source dates the current version Aug 2025. — [nhimg](https://nhimg.org/fda-ai-enabled-device-guide); [FDA PCCP guidance PDF](https://www.fda.gov/media/166704/download)
- **CDS and General Wellness guidances (6 Jan 2026)**: revised final versions issued without prior comment, replacing the 2019 and 2022 versions. CDS: most language saying time-critical software automatically fails Criterion 3 was removed, and enforcement discretion was extended to some CDS that recommends a single option to an HCP. General Wellness: new criteria for non-invasive sensing of physiologic parameters, with a wrist-worn BP example. Products using radiation or invasive technology fall outside the wellness policy. — [King & Spalding](https://www.kslaw.com/insights/articles/fda-updates-general-wellness-and-clinical-decision-support-guidance-documents); [DLA Piper, Jan 2026](https://www.dlapiper.com/en-it/insights/publications/2026/01/fda-updates-its-clinical-decision-support-and-general-wellness-guidances-key-points); [Arnold & Porter](https://www.arnoldporter.com/en/perspectives/advisories/2026/01/fda-cuts-red-tape-on-clinical-decision-support-software); [Faegre Drinker](https://faegredrinker.com/en/insights/publications/2026/1/key-updates-in-fdas-2026-general-wellness-and-clinical-decision-support-software-guidance)
- Neither 2026 guidance "expressly changes FDA's current approach to AI". The CDS guidance is silent on generative AI and chatbots. — [Honigman](https://www.honigman.com/alert-3236); [Clint Johnson analysis](https://www.clint-johnson.com/articles/fda-2026-cds-guidance-generative-ai)
- **Digital Health Advisory Committee (DHAC)**: the first meeting, 20–21 Nov 2024, covered generative-AI-enabled devices (background, not re-fetched). The second, **6 Nov 2025**, covered "Generative AI-Enabled Digital Mental Health Medical Devices" and discussed a hypothetical prescription LLM therapy chatbot. Members cited risks of hallucination, bias and sycophancy and stressed human oversight. FDA said that of more than 1,200 authorised AI devices, none involved generative AI for mental health. — [Psychiatric Times, 13 Nov 2025](https://www.psychiatrictimes.com/view/fda-committee-meets-on-generative-ai-digital-mental-health-devices); [Orrick](https://www.orrick.com/en/insights/2025/11/fdas-digital-health-advisory-committee-considers-generative-ai-therapy-chatbots-for-depression); [Hogan Lovells](https://hlc.com/en/publications/fdas-digital-health-advisory-committee-weighs-guardrails-for-generative-ai-in-mental-health-devices); [FDA meeting materials](https://www.fda.gov/media/189389/download)
- **FDA discussion paper (18 Aug 2026)**: "Considerations for the Regulation of Generative AI-Enabled Medical Devices", from the CDRH Digital Health Center of Excellence. Docket FDA-2026-N-7874, comments closed 19 Oct 2026. "This is not a draft or final guidance." Two risk axes: independence of action (non-directive information through autonomous action) and severity of consequences of incorrect output. "Talk to your doctor" disclaimers do not change an action-directing output. "Patient-facing functions may also move up the consequences axis." It proposes a voluntary Foundation Model Device Master File, says PCCPs "would extend to generative AI-enabled devices", and signals "greater reliance on postmarket monitoring". Commentary: generative-AI devices have "no established pathway to market". — [Maynard Nexsen, 26 Aug 2026](https://www.maynardnexsen.com/publication-fda-releases-discussion-paper-on-generative-ai-enabled-medical-devices-what-manufacturers-and-investors-need-to-know); [Intelacia, 31 Aug 2026](https://www.intelacia.com/2026/08/31/fda-generative-ai-medical-devices-regulation/)
- **LLM device authorisation status (conflicting)**:
  - UpDoc: 510(k) **K253281**, decided 23 Dec 2025, product code NDC "Calculator, Drug Dose", Class II, 21 CFR 868.1890. Prescription insulin-titration software with a patient app, including a "Conversation Service (UpDoc Agent)". "The FDA public record, however, does not identify a foundation model." It is marketed (Jun 2026 launch) as the first patient-facing LLM clearance. — [Innolitics, 25 Jun 2026](https://innolitics.com/articles/updoc-fda-cleared-ai-agent/); [2 Minute Medicine](https://www.2minutemedicine.com/updoc-receives-the-first-fda-clearance-for-a-patient-facing-large-language-model-in-clinical-care/); [IntuitionLabs](https://intuitionlabs.ai/articles/fda-clears-first-llm-medical-device)
  - Contradiction: The Medical Futurist (Sept 2026) says no device using generative AI or LLMs has been authorised. FDA's AI-enabled device list (reported at 1,614 entries as of 4 Sep 2026) has no LLM tag. — [Medical Futurist](https://medicalfuturist.com/the-current-state-of-fda-approved-ai-based-medical-devices); [IntuitionLabs list](https://intuitionlabs.ai/articles/fda-approved-ai-medical-devices-list) (secondary)

### Inferences
- **US pathway for IRSA**: the dose engine plus patient-facing tailored advice is unlikely to qualify as non-device CDS. Criterion 4 requires an HCP able to independently review the basis, and IRSA talks directly to patients. The wellness policy excludes radiation-related products. So IRSA would most likely be a device. With no clear predicate, De Novo is more plausible than 510(k). The LLM component would fall under the Aug 2026 discussion-paper framework: patient-facing and action-directing, placing it high on both axes. That means more premarket evidence, PCCP scoping and postmarket monitoring.
- A defensible design for any jurisdiction: keep **deterministic, validated logic** (rule engine and dose model) as the decision-maker, and use the LLM only to explain or converse within guard-rails. This mirrors how the UpDoc clearance appears to be scoped (deterministic dosing logic, conversational front-end).
- Unresolved US question: is advice protecting **third parties** (family) from the patient's radiation a "medical purpose" for the patient under FD&C Act §201(h)? The dosimetry/retention function, which bears on therapy, is the stronger device hook.

### Gaps
- FDA guidance database and Federal Register not checked directly for a final lifecycle guidance in Sep–Nov 2026.
- FDA's decision summary for K253281 not read directly.
- No information found on any FDA follow-up meeting or guidance after the comment period closed on 19 Oct 2026.

## 4. US NRC: patient release (10 CFR 35.75, RG 8.39 / NUREG-1556 Vol. 9) in 2025–2026

### Takeaway
2026 has seen major **proposed** changes, none final as of the latest sources found. Release limits would apply **per administration regimen**, so a six-cycle 177Lu-PSMA course is one regimen. Public limit 5 mSv per regimen. **Consenting, instructed caregivers up to 50 mSv per regimen**. A new 20 mSv per regimen limit for caregivers visiting non-releasable inpatients. Fewer individual dose records. ALARA removed from Part 20 in favour of a "graded approach" (EO 14300). Draft RG 8.39 Rev. 2 (DG-8070) explicitly accepts measured dose rate and patient-specific calculations as release demonstrations. The US is already moving toward the flexibility IRSA claims to unlock, while reducing paperwork.

### Cited Findings

## 6. National implementation of release rules (Euratom BSS 2013/59 and non-EU): Germany, Austria, Switzerland, UK, China, Japan

### Takeaway
Rules differ widely. **Germany (and Austria, Italy) require inpatient stay** after 177Lu RLT. In Germany this is at least about 48 h, until the dose rate at 2 m keeps public dose under 1 mSv/a, under §122(4) StrlSchV. Switzerland also uses about 48 h for 177Lu-PSMA. The UK has given outpatient 177Lu-DOTATATE since 2012. **China** has no specific national standard for 177Lu-DOTATATE and local norms vary. **Japan** uses product manuals with dose-rate or cumulative-dose criteria, and a 2025 JSCO policy paper calls for new 177Lu-PSMA discharge guidance. IRSA's claim to enable "more flexible discharge" only works where the rule is dose-based rather than fixed-duration. Germany would need a legal or guideline change.

### Cited Findings
- **EU framework**: Directive 2013/59/Euratom requires dose constraints for carers and comforters and protection of the public. Implementation is national. — [EUR-Lex 2013/59/Euratom](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/2013/59/oj) (article-level wording not re-verified this session)
- **Germany**: patients stay "at least 48 h until the dose rate (measured at a distance of 2 m) is below a level warranting the dose to the public to stay below 1 mSv per year". The dose rate must be measured and documented at discharge. — [Kurth et al., EJNMMI Res 2018;8:32](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s13550-018-0386-4)
- Germany: §122(4) StrlSchV allows release from nuclear-medicine wards only if the expected effective dose to the public stays within limits. A 2025/2026 Thieme study notes no specific 177Lu discharge guidelines beyond the general approach and aims to support binding 177Lu criteria. — [Thieme, doi:10.1055/a-2784-7468](https://www.thieme-connect.com/products/ejournals/html/10.1055/a-2784-7468) (from search snippet; page blocked); [StrlSchV text](https://gesetze-in-app.de/StrlSchV/)
- **Germany, Austria, Italy**: "legal regulations ... demand hospitalization" of 177Lu-PSMA patients, in contrast to other countries. — [PMC5897276](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5897276)
- **Switzerland**: BAG guidance table lists 177Lu (PSMA) with release 48 h after application (reference activity listed as 1000 MBq). Not verified in detail. — [BAG Wegleitung Radionuklidtherapie](https://www.bag.admin.ch/dam/bag/de/dokumente/str/str-wegleitungen/bauliche-anforderungen/radionuklidtherapie.pdf.download.pdf/Strahlenschutz_Wegleitung_Radionuklidtherapie_DE.pdf)
- **UK**: outpatient 177Lu-DOTATATE PRRT since 2012 at a London centre, with precautions designed to meet UK dose limits. Earlier state: a 2014 NRC survey reported UK same-day release under 800 MBq. — [Radiation precautions for inpatient and outpatient 177Lu-DOTATATE (PMC6484059)](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6484059/); [NRC memo 2014](https://www.nrc.gov/docs/ML1421/ML14217A350.pdf)
- **China**: institutions follow varying local norms for 177Lu-DOTATATE hospitalisation and discharge, with "no specific national radiation protection standard". - **Japan**: a 2025 JSCO working-group policy says 177Lu discharge can follow patient-specific cumulative-dose calculation. The only usable criteria are the product manuals (oxodotreotide and PSMA-617), and new guidance is needed. The DOTATATE manual (2nd ed.) gives a dose-rate criterion at 1 m, reported as 10 µSv/h; check against the manual. — [Int J Clin Oncol 2025, s10147-025-02858-3](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10147-025-02858-3); [Japanese Lu-177-DOTATATE manual (PMC5852188)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5852188/)
- Cross-European comparison: dose-rate release limits such as 40 µSv/h and 25 µSv/h at 1 m are cited, and stays range from hours to 24–72 h. — [search synthesis of PMC6484059 and related](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6484059/)
- US outpatient 177Lu-PSMA feasibility is documented. — [PMC11619580](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11619580/)

### Inferences
- **IRSA's flexibility claim depends on jurisdiction.** In dose-based regimes (US, UK, Japan's cumulative-dose approach, possibly China once standardised), better patient-specific data could justify earlier or outpatient release. In Germany, Austria and Switzerland, the about 48 h stay is set by regulation or guidance, so a device cannot override it. Authorities would have to amend rules, likely based on prospective dosimetric evidence that IRSA could help generate. China's lack of a national standard is an opening for IRSA-style standardised behavioural guidance (the 2024 study's own recommendation).

## 8. Liability and responsibility for incorrect AI advice (for example, "safe to see grandchildren")

### Takeaway
In the EU the **new Product Liability Directive (EU) 2024/2853** treats software and AI as products under strict liability. It applies to products placed on the market **after 9 Dec 2026**, three weeks from now. The proposed AI Liability Directive was **withdrawn** in 2025. An IRSA manufacturer would therefore face strict liability for defective advice harming a family member. Separately, the radiation-protection licensee or undertaking (the hospital) keeps regulatory responsibility for release decisions. No 2026 legal analysis specific to AI-generated radiation-release advice was found.

### Cited Findings
- PLD 2024/2853 entered into force 8 Dec 2024. Transposition deadline **9 Dec 2026**. Products placed on the market after that date fall under it, earlier products under the old PLD. Software and AI-integrated products are "products". Strict, no-fault liability. AI system providers count as "manufacturers". — [Orrick, Jun 2025](https://www.orrick.com/en/Insights/2025/06/New-EU-Directive-Modernizes-Defective-Product-Liability-to-Address-New-Technologies); [Taylor Wessing](https://www.taylorwessing.com/de/synapse/2025/ai-in-life-sciences/new-product-liability-risks-for-ai-products); [Gibson Dunn PLD paper, Mar 2026](https://www.gibsondunn.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/eu-product-liability-directive-responding-to-software-ai-and-complex-supply-chains.pdf); [CMS](https://cms.law/en/int/publication/the-new-eu-product-liability-directive-eu-2024-2853)
- Bird & Bird (2026) describes "expanded liability, challenging defences, and new evidentiary burdens" for AI under the new PLD. Page blocked (403), so only the title was confirmed. — [Bird & Bird 2026](https://cm.twobirds.com/en/insights/2026/france/ai-liability-in-light-of-the-new-2024-pld-expanded-liability-challenging-defences-and-new-evidentiar)
- AI Liability Directive proposal COM(2022) 496 withdrawn. One source gives the Commission decision as 16 Jul 2025 and the OJ notice as 6 Oct 2025. That leaves the AI Act plus the new PLD as the framework. — [search synthesis incl. Kennedys](https://kennedyslaw.com/en/a-new-liability-framework-for-products-and-ai); [Shaping Tomorrow scan, Jul 2026](https://decision-intel.shapingtomorrow.com/scans/regulation-standards-policy-change/2026-07-06-ex-post-liability-turn/scan.html)
- US: under 10 CFR 35.75 the **licensee** authorises release and must give instructions. The proposed rule keeps licensee-centred responsibility through written release procedures and consent. — [DRPS](https://www.diagnosticradphys.com/physicspulse/medical-health-physics/nrc-2026-rulemaking-wave-medical-use/)
- FDA's 2026 discussion paper says disclaimers ("talk to your doctor") do not change the regulatory character of an action-directing output. — [Maynard Nexsen](https://www.maynardnexsen.com/publication-fda-releases-discussion-paper-on-generative-ai-enabled-medical-devices-what-manufacturers-and-investors-need-to-know)

### Inferences
- **Layered accountability for a wrong "safe to see grandchildren" output:**
  - (a) Manufacturer: strict product liability in the EU (PLD) and, in the US, state product-liability law.
  - (b) Hospital or licensee: regulatory responsibility for release and instructions (NRC licensee; under the BSS, the undertaking or practitioner), plus malpractice or negligence for deploying and supervising the tool.
  - (c) The AI Act deployer duties (human oversight, monitoring) fall on the hospital from 2028.
  - (d) A foundation-model provider is unlikely to be directly liable to the patient but may face contractual or recourse claims.
  - (e) A regulator receiving IRSA data could arguably take on duties once it uses the data for oversight. Untested.
- Third-party victims (family members) can sue under the PLD. Under the new PLD this does not depend on a contract.
- Mitigations: keep the LLM non-directive, generate numeric "safe distance/time" outputs from the validated rule engine with conservative defaults, log every advice event (also required under AI Act logging), and route edge cases to staff.

### Gaps

## 9. Patent landscape: wearable dosimeter plus app or assistant for patient release

### Takeaway
Targeted searches of Google Patents and the web found **no patent or application combining a patient-worn dosimeter, a therapy-specific dose model, and an app or chatbot giving post-discharge release guidance**. Adjacent prior art exists in smartphone-linked dosimeters, patient-specific RPT dosimetry, and academic wearable or self-measurement systems for RPT patients. **EP26199231.7 cannot be verified**: an EP application filed in 2026 would normally be published only 18 months after its priority date. This was not an exhaustive freedom-to-operate search.

### Cited Findings
- EP2724520A1: mobile radio unit (smartphone) with built-in dosimeter-radiometer and dose-level alerts. Not patient or radiopharmaceutical specific. — [Google Patents EP2724520A1](http://www.google.com/patents/EP2724520A1?cl=en)
- US9151848B2: dosimetry system in which dosimeters send data to cell phones running an app, with central collection. — [Google Patents US9151848B2](https://patents.google.com/patent/US9151848B2/en)

- CA2331064A1: patient-specific dosimetry for radiopharmaceutical dosing (lean body mass, pre-therapy dosimetry). No home monitoring. — [Google Patents CA2331064A1](https://patents.google.com/patent/CA2331064A1/en)

### Inferences
- Novelty is likely to rest on the **combination**: a patient-worn sensor, an AI surrogate dose model, a jurisdiction-specific rule engine, a neuro-symbolic RAG LLM, and regulator reporting. Individual elements are well anticipated by the prior art above, so inventive step will be contested. Software and AI claims at the EPO also need a technical effect. The concept paper's own publication (Nov 2026) may limit later filings if it predates their priority.
- Because WIDMApp and similar academic wearable systems exist, any IRSA freedom-to-operate review should check their patent families, which this search did not.
