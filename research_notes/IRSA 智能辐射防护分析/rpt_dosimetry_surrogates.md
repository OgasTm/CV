# Wearable dosimetry after RPT and AI/physics surrogates for MC dosimetry and external-exposure estimation (2025–2026 state of the art, as it bears on IRSA)

Scope note: Searched Europe PMC/PubMed (REST API; full texts of open-access papers parsed), SNMMI/JNM supplement pages, arXiv, Google Patents and vendor pages, as of 2026-11-19. Items labelled "baseline/prior art" are pre-2025. Items marked "[snippet only]" were seen only as abstract/search summaries, not in full text. I found no MICCAI 2026 or EANM 2026 congress papers/abstracts specific to these topics (see Gaps).

## 1. Wearable / continuous patient-worn detectors in RPT; commercial devices; links to discharge guidance; 177Lu vs 131I detector issues

### Takeaway
Patient-worn or room-mounted continuous detectors in RPT exist mostly as research prototypes aimed at *dosimetry* (time-activity curves) rather than *radiation protection of contacts*. The only 2026 peer-reviewed system that streams real-time data for discharge planning is ceiling-mounted, not worn (Bailey 2026, 131I, n=250). No 2025–2026 paper was found linking a patient-worn dosimeter plus app to individualized family/public guidance. However, a 2016 IBM patent (US9972189B1) already claims a dosimeter on a nuclear-medicine patient, linked to a smartphone, that issues precaution alerts and warns high-risk contacts. That patent is directly relevant prior art for the IRSA patent's novelty.

### Cited Findings
**Continuous / wearable detectors (2026)**
- Bailey DL et al. (2026, Asia Ocean J Nucl Med Biol): ceiling-mounted GM detector (AustralRad PMS, Globalsonics; LND7231 GM tube, 30 g tube, 150 g unit, Power-over-Ethernet), mounted ~1.8 m above the bed in 131I isolation rooms. It sampled every minute over 1–3 days after 1–6 GBq 131I. Effective retention half-time 11.9 ± 3.2 h (range 5.0–23.1 h; n=250); 11.5 h if <55 y vs 14.4 h if ≥55 y (P=0.0007). The authors state the real-time data can support discharge planning and "live" staff updates. — [Bailey 2026, PMC12854189](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12854189/)
- Same paper, on limitations: the detector was calibrated with 137Cs to read Sv/h at 1 m, with "no compensation ... for the different distance (1.8 m)" or for "a distributed source volume rather than a point source". Patient movement and bathroom visits produce "discrete, stepwise changes" in readings. Only *relative* retention was reliable. — [Bailey 2026](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12854189/)
- Bailey also cites earlier similar systems: discrete-time-point ceiling detectors (10.5 h t½eff with rhTSH, n=36; 15.7 h after T4 withdrawal, n=236) and Kääriä et al. with 1-min sampling (median t½eff 12.6 h). — [Bailey 2026](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12854189/)
- Cucurachi N et al. (2026, PLoS One): portable gamma detectors placed on the patient during injection gave real-time dose-rate–time curves in 885 PET exams and 15 177Lu-DOTATATE therapies. They derived thresholds for extravasation (Δp_innor = 0.30; ΔR_t = 388 µSv/h); one therapeutic extravasation had an estimated 11.6 Gy local self-dose. This shows on-body detectors with a fixed short-term geometry working in routine care. — [Cucurachi 2026, doi:10.1371/journal.pone.0350116](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0350116)

**Wearable dosimetry prototypes for TACs (baseline/prior art, mostly simulation or phantom)**
- WIDMApp (Morganti S et al., Med Phys 2021): multi-channel wearable detector system; proof of concept by Monte Carlo only. It reconstructs organ time-activity curves from time-count curves of several small detectors, intended to reduce imaging time points. — [Morganti 2021, PMC9298698](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9298698/)
- MD-PHD vest (SNMMI 2019 abstract, JNM 60 suppl 1:313): 15–20 small detectors placed according to the patient's anatomy, plus one 24 h SPECT/CT, for home tracking of 177Lu-DOTATATE organ washout. Initial simulation only; planned 2-min daily acquisitions for 7 (up to 21) days. — [JNM abstract](https://jnm.snmjournals.org/content/60/supplement_1/313.abstract); [BioTechniques](https://biotechniques.com/cancer-research/a-vest-is-of-interest-for-personalized-radionucleotide-therapy) [snippet only]
- "Wearable, Integrated Sparse-SPECT for Continuous, Real-Time Dosimetry in Cancer RPT" (Lall, UC Berkeley EECS Tech Report 2025): sparse uncollimated mm-scale gamma-sensing chips for continuous activity reconstruction. — [Berkeley EECS 2025-31720](https://www2.eecs.berkeley.edu/Pubs/TechRpts/2025/31720.html) [snippet only]
- VASTRA (CIRMS 2023 abstract): CMOS-based wearable for patient-specific temporal activity data; validated only in a "smart phantom". — [CIRMS 2023](https://cirms.org/wp-content/uploads/cirms2023/general-abstracts/CIRMS2023_Abstract-Rao_Papineni.pdf) [snippet only]
- Review: Strigari L et al. (Sensors 2023), "Evolution of Portable Sensors for In-Vivo Dose and Time-Activity Curve Monitoring". It notes that time points per patient are limited by compliance and scanner availability. — [Strigari 2023, PMC10007630](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10007630/) [snippet only]

**131I collar detectors (baseline/prior art)**
- Brinks P et al., "Measuring the actual I-131 thyroid uptake curve with a collar detector system: a feasibility study", Eur J Nucl Med Mol Imaging 2017. — [doi:10.1007/s00259-016-3595-y](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00259-016-3595-y) [title only]
- van Gils K et al., "A method to measure the absorbed dose of the thyroid during I-131 therapy, using a collar detector system and a SPECT acquisition", Med Phys 2017. — [doi:10.1002/mp.12472](https://doi.org/10.1002/mp.12472) [title only]

Pilot (NCT03517579) in Graves' disease and thyroid cancer; it was "accurate and feasible for use outside of a healthcare facility" and lateralised remnant tissue. — [Santhanam 2021, PMC8446004](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8446004/)

**Consumer and smartphone sensors**
- O'Callaghan J et al. (Sensors 2022): a smartphone-operated GM sensor (Smart Geiger Pro, Technonia) for at-home 131I pretherapy dosimetry. Tested factors: activity, photon energy, patient size, placement and motion. Performance was "comparable" to a hospital thyroid uptake probe in 4 patients; the authors set out recommended conditions of use. — [O'Callaghan 2022, doi:10.3390/s22239392](https://doi.org/10.3390/s22239392)
- Phone-camera "radiation apps" are not usable as personal dosimeters. — [Sci Rep 2021](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-021-92195-y) [snippet only]

**Patents (prior art for IRSA's EP26199231.7)**

Assignees not verified. — [US10722171](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/10722171); [US12377289](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/12377289) [snippet only]

**Photon physics relevant to detector choice**
- 177Lu gamma emissions are 113 keV (6.2%) and 208 keV (10.4%); clearance is mainly urinary, mostly within 48 h. — [Levart 2025, PMC12595148](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12595148/)
- 131I: Monte Carlo (EGSnrc) representative ambient-dose-equivalent rate constant Γ_H*(10) = 63.6 ± 0.1 µSv m² GBq⁻¹ h⁻¹. Published values differ by −17% to +22%. — [Cortez 2026, J Radiol Prot, doi:10.1088/1361-6498/ae80d4](https://doi.org/10.1088/1361-6498/ae80d4)

### Inferences
- **Novelty:** The "wearable dosimeter + smartphone + precaution alerts for NM patients" idea is prior art (IBM 2016). The defensible novelty of IRSA would sit in three places: (i) the physics/MC-surrogate inversion from an *on-body* signal to retained activity and to dose at a contact's distance; (ii) uncertainty propagation with out-of-range fallback; (iii) clinical validation. The patent claims should be checked against US9972189B1.
- **Sensitivity:** Counting sensitivity is unlikely to be the limiting factor for an on-body detector. Contact dose rates are ~120–150 µSv/h at discharge for 177Lu (150 µSv/h at 0.1 m per Levart 2025; 12.2 mrem/h ≈ 122 µSv/h on contact per Tipnis 2026), far above background (~0.1 µSv/h). Even after several effective half-lives, rates stay well above typical EPD lower limits.
- **Geometry:** The dominant error source is the source–detector geometry. A torso- or wrist-worn detector "sees" nearby organs (bladder, kidneys, liver, bulky tumours) disproportionately. Bladder filling and voiding cause large swings, and wrist position changes with arm posture. Bailey 2026 shows step artefacts even for a fixed ceiling detector at 1.8 m. Expect much larger variance at contact distance. This is exactly where an MC-trained mapping, using SPECT-informed biodistribution and the detector position, could add value, but it also requires motion and bladder-state handling (an accelerometer, or a fixed measurement posture as in the earlier ceiling-detector protocol where patients lay on the bed at each measurement).
- **Energy response:** For 177Lu the primary lines (113/208 keV) are within EPD rated ranges, but tissue-scattered spectra at contact shift toward lower energies. Uncompensated GM tubes (e.g., smartphone Geiger sensors) over-respond at low energy relative to a 137Cs calibration. A nuclide- and geometry-specific calibration (phantom with 177Lu and with 131I) is needed. Energy-compensated Si-diode EPDs within IEC-type −29%/+67% bands would still give errors of tens of percent unless recalibrated. A Lu-177 vs 131I dual calibration is mandatory.

### Gaps
- No 2025–2026 peer-reviewed study was found of a *patient-worn* dosimeter used to individualize post-discharge guidance for contacts (177Lu or 131I).
- No 2025–2026 paper was found that quantifies EPD/GM energy response specifically for 177Lu patient (scattered) spectra at low dose rates. I could not verify IEC 61526 clause text or Mirion/Thermo specifications.
- I could not verify the assignees or claims of US10722171 / US12377289. A search of EP/WO family members for wearable RPT dosimetry was not done.

## 2. Measured exposure to family members/public after 177Lu-PSMA/DOTATATE and 131I (2025–2026); typical doses vs 1 mSv/5 mSv; are rules over-conservative?

### Takeaway
2025–2026 measurements consistently show family doses far below constraints. For 177Lu-DOTATATE under UK restrictions, the median was 0.038 mSv over 4 weeks (n=12). For 177Lu-PSMA caregivers, doses were roughly 0.02–1.2 mSv. Multicentre German data show 177Lu effective half-lives 54–74% shorter than physical. Regulatory calculations that use physical half-life (US NRC default) and unattenuated point sources are therefore systematically conservative. Because measurements were taken *under* existing restrictions, the data support relaxing generic rules only with individualized monitoring, which is IRSA's premise.

### Cited Findings
- **Levart D et al. (2025, EJNMMI Phys; King's College Hospital)**, 177Lu-DOTATATE, n=12 adult family members wearing TLDs continuously for 4 weeks. Median effective dose 0.038 mSv after a median 7515 MBq administered. Patients were discharged at a mean 6.2 h. Median dose rates at discharge: 150 µSv/h at 0.1 m and 15 µSv/h at 1 m (anterior abdomen), corresponding to a median retention of 35.8% by quantitative SPECT/CT. Over 4 weeks, family members spent a median 39.5 h at 1 m and 19.9 h at <1 m. — [Levart 2025, PMC12595148](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12595148/)
- Levart, restrictions in force: no contact <1 m with adults for 6 days; no bed sharing for 16 days; child-specific periods of 17, 13 and 10 days for ages <2, 2–5 and 5–11 y. These derive from a prior study combining retention, dose-rate measurements and social-contact models, against a 1 mSv per cycle constraint for family (keeping them <5 mSv in 5 years under UK IRR17) and 0.3 mSv for the public. The authors explicitly say results "may also be used as an indication as to whether the existing advice is overly cautious", and that standardized rules "may not be appropriate for all individuals" because of disease burden, avidity, biodistribution, renal function and contact patterns. The two highest family doses were linked to higher patient retention or longer close contact. — [Levart 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12595148/)
- **Schütze C et al. (2026, Nuklearmedizin)**, retrospective, 8 German centres, 210 dose-rate measurement sets, mean 7149 ± 522 MBq. Effective half-life from fits to ≤168 h (mean): Pluvicto 1.60 d, Lutathera 1.89 d, in-house PSMA 2.10 d, in-house RRT 2.85 d. Fits to ≤48 h give shorter values (1.30, 1.40, 1.45, 1.97 d), statistically significant except Lutathera. Ranges were wide (e.g., ihPSMA 0.35–6.65 d). Median deviation from physical half-life: −54% to −74%. Recommended conservative (90th-percentile) T½eff for discharge calculations: 2.0 d Pluvicto, 3.0 d Lutathera, 3.5 d ihPSMA, 4.0 d ihRRT. The authors note there are currently no binding German discharge criteria for 177Lu. — [Schütze 2026, PMC13046430](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13046430/)
- **Sheikhzadeh P et al. (2025, Radiat Prot Dosimetry)**, 177Lu-PSMA, 47 patients, dose rates up to 168 h. Caregivers' effective doses were 20–1170 µSv. Dose rate at 1 m fell below 30 µSv/h within 2–3 h and below 20 µSv/h after 6 h. — [PubMed 40434013 / doi:10.1093/rpd/ncaf059](https://doi.org/10.1093/rpd/ncaf059) [snippet only]
- **Takano S et al. (2025, Ann Nucl Med)**, Japanese 177Lu-PSMA-617 PK/dosimetry study: external exposure to 7 family members living with patients was "minimal"; the authors caution about interpreting very low readings. — [doi:10.1007/s12149-025-02079-8](https://doi.org/10.1007/s12149-025-02079-8) [snippet only]
- **Tipnis SV, Rieter WJ (2026, J Nucl Med Technol)**, 10 infusions of 7.4 GBq 177Lu-DOTATATE: on contact 12.2 ± 4.9 mrem/h; at 1 m 1.6 ± 1.0 mrem/h; cumulative 133.8 ± 19.3 mrem at contact and 39.4 ± 10.1 mrem at the armrest (~0.2 m) during infusion. The authors call this "well below" the NRC 500 mrem release criterion. — [Tipnis 2026, doi:10.2967/jnmt.126.272633](https://tech.snmjournals.org/content/early/2026/08/18/jnmt.126.272633)
- **Swinson A et al. (SNMMI 2026 Annual Meeting abstract, JNM 67 suppl 1)**: technologist workflow for 177Lu release under 10 CFR 35.75 (5 mSv TEDE limit; written instructions if >1 mSv). It uses occupancy factor 0.25 at 1 m and the *physical* half-life (6.65 d): TEDE (mSv) ≈ 0.576 × dose rate at 1 m (mR/h), with recheck time t = T_p × log2(DR_now/DR_limit). It mentions a screen of ~5 mR/h at 1 m and a "potential new 2.2 mR/h @ 1 m" simplified pathway in draft guidance summaries (not tied to a specific NRC document). Worked example: 198.2 mCi, 3.2 mR/h at 4.5 h, giving an ~86 h hold. — [JNM 67 suppl 1:26158](https://jnm.snmjournals.org/content/67/supplement_1/26158)
- Earlier baseline: Demir et al. (177Lu-PSMA): 23 caregivers, mean total 202.3 ± 42.7 µSv (range 120–265 µSv). — [Semantic Scholar entry](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/Evaluation-of-radiation-safety-in-177Lu-PSMA-and-of-Demir-Abuqbeitah/3759833246227b0a8a86f3e4c9d8676b452359b9) [snippet only]
- 131I: Bailey 2026 found t½eff of 11.9 h (n=250) for DTC ablation in hospital. The physical half-life of 131I is ~8 d, so physical-decay-based calculations are highly conservative for ablation patients. — [Bailey 2026](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12854189/)
- Levart notes that individualized 6 h and 24 h dose-rate measurements have been proposed (prior work) to produce individualized radiation protection recommendations, and that measurement-derived contact restrictions have long been used for outpatient 131I hyperthyroidism therapy. — [Levart 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12595148/)
- Takai G et al. (2024, Ann Nucl Med; baseline): pre-therapy 111In-SRS activity predicted the 1-m dose-equivalent rate ~18 h after 177Lu-DOTATATE (abdominal ROI r = 0.63; whole-body r = 0.56; n=21). This is an early example of predicting release-relevant dose rate from pre-therapy imaging, analogous to IRSA's optional PET/CT initialization. Only moderate correlation was achieved. — [Takai 2024, doi:10.1007/s12149-024-01962-0](https://doi.org/10.1007/s12149-024-01962-0)

### Inferences
- **Dose-rate constant (computed from Levart):** median retained activity ≈ 0.358 × 7515 ≈ 2.69 GBq gives 15 µSv/h at 1 m. That is ≈5.6 µSv h⁻¹ GBq⁻¹ at 1 m, anterior, body-attenuated. This is a useful sanity check for any IRSA model.
- **Margin under current rules:** the median family dose (0.038 mSv per 4 weeks) is ~4% of the 1 mSv per cycle constraint (Levart). Even the 177Lu-PSMA caregiver maximum (1.17 mSv; Sheikhzadeh) is around the per-episode public-type constraint and well below 5 mSv carers/comforters limits. Generic rules therefore carry large average margins, but tails exist (high retention, long close contact). Personalized monitoring could shorten restrictions for most patients while catching the tail.
- **Conservatism of the NRC default:** the effective-half-life evidence (Schütze) implies the NRC-default physical-half-life integration overstates integrated dose by roughly T_phys/T_eff ≈ 6.65/1.6 ≈ 4× for Pluvicto, using the median-based mean. Using the conservative 90th percentile (2.0 d), the factor is still ≈3×. An IRSA device that measures each patient's own T_eff captures most of the achievable accuracy gain without any MC.

### Gaps
- No 2025–2026 study with *caregiver* dosimeters after 131I thyroid cancer therapy (home phase) was found in full text. Only in-hospital retention (Bailey 2026) and the MC body attenuation factor (Cortez 2026) were found.
- No 2026 multicentre family-dose study for 177Lu-PSMA in full text was found. Sheikhzadeh 2025 and Takano 2025 details are unverified beyond abstracts.
- Whether the "2.2 mR/h at 1 m" pathway in Swinson 2026 corresponds to an official NRC RG 8.39 Phase 2 draft is unverified.

## 3. Deep-learning / AI surrogates for Monte Carlo dosimetry (2025–2026), GPU/modern MC, external dose-rate field estimation

### Takeaway
2025–2026 AI dosimetry work targets *internal absorbed dose* (voxel dose kernels, voxel dose maps, single-time-point corrections). Reported accuracy is typically ~5–15% vs MC, with internal validation only. Several papers show severe failure outside the training domain. I found no published AI surrogate for the *external* dose-rate field around a patient (dose to a bystander at a given distance and posture). The closest work is MC- or phantom-derived body-attenuation and self-absorption factors, which are simple scalars. IRSA's "MC-trained surrogate for external exposure" is therefore novel in application, but the physics problem (photon transport in air plus body self-shielding) is low-dimensional. Analytical or lookup approaches may suffice.

### Cited Findings
**Internal-dosimetry DL surrogates (2025–2026)**
- Ha S et al. (2026, Phys Med Biol): DL predicts beta-emitter voxel dose kernels from CT density plus the energy spectrum, then convolves them with TIA. Kernel R² vs MC: 0.99 (166Ho) and 0.91 (177Lu, zero-shot). Patient-level computation fell from 4.6 h (MC) to 2.6 min. 177Lu test error: 2.1 Gy (11.6%). A conventional DL model trained on 90Y images gave 129.4 Gy (657.4%) error on 177Lu, a stark out-of-domain failure. Mean tumour-dose error for 166Ho: 1.1 Gy (2.9%) vs VSV 7.2–7.3% and OLINDA 4.5%. — [Ha 2026, doi:10.1088/1361-6560/ae5375](https://doi.org/10.1088/1361-6560/ae5375)
- Banala et al. (2026 review) note that the clinical 177Lu test in Ha et al. included only 2 patients. — [Banala 2026, PMC13559226](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13559226/)
- Kapis OE et al. (2026, J Appl Clin Med Phys): GAN maps CT density kernels (15×15×15) to 177Lu dose voxel kernels trained on GATE MC. Kernel accuracy 98.2% (water), 98.3% (soft tissue), 96.9% (muscle), 94.2% (cortical bone). Phantom Bland–Altman LoA −0.59 to +0.44 Gy vs GATE. Phantom only. — [Kapis 2026, PMC13269658](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13269658/)
- Ennassiri H (2026, Biomed Phys Eng Express): OpenGATE 10 radial 177Lu dose point kernels in 12 media. Ridge/Lasso/ElasticNet surrogates using density and elemental composition did **not** beat a simple water-kernel resampling baseline (ElasticNet LOTO MAPE mean 224%, median 117%). Errors reached thousands of percent in lung-like media; an out-of-distribution low-density medium degraded severely. A negative result: naive ML surrogates can be worse than simple physics scaling. — [Ennassiri 2026, doi:10.1088/2057-1976/aea427](https://doi.org/10.1088/2057-1976/aea427)
- Mansouri Z et al. (2024, EJNMMI; baseline): hybrid transformer for 177Lu-DOTATATE voxel dose maps from SPECT/CT, 22 patients/50 sessions, voxel relative absolute error 5.28 ± 1.32% vs MC, internal 5-fold CV only. — [doi:10.1007/s00259-024-06618-9](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00259-024-06618-9); metrics per [Banala 2026 table](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13559226/)
- Jalilifar M et al. (2025, Sci Rep): MLP-based 131I dosimetry from a single oblique planar view. External 23-patient cohort; organ differences −14.8% to +11.2% vs GATE v8.2 MC. — [doi:10.1038/s41598-025-09212-7](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-025-09212-7); metrics per [Banala 2026](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13559226/)
- Semi-supervised, PBPK-regularized cGAN for pre-therapy voxel dose prediction (synthetic study, ~1,000 phantoms): organ-dose MAPE 9–11%, no clinical validation. — [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/399709081_Semi-supervised_learning_for_dose_prediction_in_targeted_radionuclide_therapy_a_synthetic_data_study); metrics per [Banala 2026](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13559226/)
- DL voxel-wise adjustment of single-time-point 177Lu-PSMA dosimetry (2026; 317 SPECT/CT scans, 21 patients): voxel-wise NRMSE within 15.0% for all organs and tumours. — [ScienceDirect S3051291326002557](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S3051291326002557) [snippet only]
- Swin UNETR prediction of 68Ga-FAPI-46 PET/CT dose-rate maps (2026, BMC Med Imaging). — [doi:10.1186/s12880-026-02489-8](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12880-026-02489-8) [title only]
- Banala et al. (2026, Front Nucl Med) review conclusion: across PSMA and SSTR dosimetry, AI evidence is "early pretherapy dose prediction, dose-map ... reports" with "limited external reference-based testing", "sparse external validation, uncertainty reporting, and threshold-based agreement analysis". No prospective activity-prescription trial exists. — [Banala 2026](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13559226/)
- Yazdani E, ..., Zaidi H (2026, Theranostics) review of dosimetry simplification: MC "take[s] hours or days", motivating DL (Sec. 4.6) and DT/PBPK (Sec. 4.8). It describes hybrid physics–DL approaches, including residual learning to correct SPECT-blurred dose toward high-resolution MC (the DblurDoseNet-type approach), and Götz et al.'s DNN + empirical-mode-decomposition DVKs (26 177Lu-PSMA patients; 52,274 density kernels). It flags "sensitivity to out-of-distribution anatomy" and cross-centre generalization as unsolved. — [Yazdani 2026, PMC13549228](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13549228/)

**MC engines (reference standard for training and validating surrogates)**
- GATE 10 (Sarrut D et al., Phys Med Biol 2026, Part I): Geant4-based; Python interface, enhanced multithreading and multiprocessing, embeddable as a library. Suitable for generating IRSA training data (patient phantom with biodistribution, detector on torso or wrist, bystander dosimetry). — [Sarrut 2026, PMC13344643](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13344643/)
- A commercial MC voxel engine (MIM SurePlan MRT) for 177Lu-DOTATATE (2026, 5 patients/20 cycles) showed generally small differences vs VSV dosimetry for most organs and tumours. — [ScienceDirect S3051291326002727](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S3051291326002727) [snippet only]
- Collapsed-cone superposition validated for whole-body 177Lu-PSMA-617 dosimetry (Terro A et al., Med Phys 2025), a fast deterministic alternative to MC. — [Terro 2025, PMC12409110](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12409110/) [title only]

**External dose-rate around the patient (closest work to IRSA's external-exposure engine)**
- Cortez E et al. (2026, J Radiol Prot): EGSnrc MC of an adult male voxel phantom with biodistributed 131I gives a body attenuation factor (BAF) of 0.729 ± 0.002 at 1 m (F-18 FDG check: 0.628). The authors frame this as improving shielding and caregiver/family dose assessments for 131I therapy. — [Cortez 2026](https://doi.org/10.1088/1361-6498/ae80d4)
- Ierace J et al. (2026, Phys Eng Sci Med): ionisation-chamber measurements of point, line and phantom (NEMA body; PMMA neck) sources for Tc-99m, F-18, I-131 (127.5–162.7 MBq capsules) and Lu-177 (1718–2147 MBq point/line; 2600 MBq in phantom) at 10–100 cm. Gamma factors agreed with literature within 1–2% (I-131 0.721 µSv/h/MBq at 30 cm). Self-absorption factor for F-18 was 0.71 ± 0.04, consistent with MC. They conclude point-source/inverse-square assumptions "overestimate exposures at clinical distances", especially at short range. — [Ierace 2026, PMC13638693](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13638693/)
- Ierace background: NRC RG 8.39 calculations neglect body attenuation, redistribution and excretion. Willegaignon et al. and Yi et al. found calculated rates exceeded patient measurements regardless of source model, and proposed self-shielding correction factors. Siegel proposed line-source models, and Broggio found the line source a reasonable approximation for Tc-99m, F-18 and I-131. — [Ierace 2026](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13638693/)
- A simplified feline phantom + MC for staff exposure during [211At]NaAt veterinary treatment (2026) shows MC-plus-simple-phantom use for third-party exposure. — [Front Vet Sci 2026, doi:10.3389/fvets.2026.1897774](https://doi.org/10.3389/fvets.2026.1897774) [title only]

### Inferences
- **Where AI can help, and where it cannot:** surrogate accuracy of ~5–15% vs MC is the realistic expectation, based on the internal-dosimetry literature. For external exposure, the physics is dominated by inverse-square, a body attenuation factor of ~0.6–0.75, and source extent at <0.5 m. A small set of MC-derived lookup tables (BAF by nuclide, distance, orientation, BMI) may match an NN surrogate. The genuinely hard, learnable part is the *inverse problem*: from an on-body detector reading (wrist/torso, posture-dependent) plus optional SPECT/PET to whole-body retained activity and its spatial distribution.
- **Generalization risk:** Ha 2026 (657% error out of domain) and Ennassiri 2026 (linear surrogates worse than a water baseline; OOD collapse) are strong citations for IRSA's out-of-range fallback design. They also argue for physics-structured surrogates (kernel/BAF corrections on top of analytic models) rather than black-box regressors.
- **Training-data generation:** GATE 10 (Python, library mode) is a natural engine: XCAT/ICRP-type phantoms with nuclide-specific biodistributions, a detector model at the torso or wrist, and H*(10)/Hp(10) scoring at bystander positions.

### Gaps
- No MICCAI 2026, IEEE TMI/TRPMS 2026 or EANM 2026 paper on AI surrogates for *external* dose-rate fields around RPT patients was found. MICCAI 2026 proceedings were not reachable via search.
- No 2025–2026 GPU-MC paper specific to RPT (e.g., MCGPU, GGEMS) was retrieved. The Yazdani review mentions GPU-accelerated MC only generically.
- DblurDoseNet (Li Z, Dewaraja Y et al., Med Phys ~2022) was not verified directly; it is referenced only through Yazdani's description of residual learning for SPECT blur.

## 4. Uncertainty quantification for dosimetry surrogates (conformal prediction, Bayesian/ensembles, OOD detection)

### Takeaway
2026 brought the first split-conformal application to DL dose prediction (radiotherapy, arXiv, Nov 2026) and a calibrated MC-dropout / mean-variance / deep-ensemble comparison for an NN dose engine with uncertainty thresholds that reject high-risk predictions. Both map directly onto IRSA's "uncertainty interval + conservative fallback" design. No RPT- or external-exposure-specific conformal or OOD paper was found.

### Cited Findings
- Brodeur R, Charest A-S, Archambault L (arXiv:2611.14438, submitted 10 Nov 2026 to PMB): "first application of split conformal prediction" to DL dose prediction (head-and-neck, deep ensemble). *Maximum aggregation* of voxelwise nonconformity scores gives simultaneous coverage of all voxels, with conservative, wide intervals. *Quantile aggregation* guarantees a minimum fraction of covered voxels per future patient, with narrower intervals. Groupwise calibration and uncertainty scaling narrowed intervals in some regions. Empirical coverage was "consistent with nominal levels" (no numbers in the abstract). — [arXiv:2611.14438](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14438)
- Schneider M et al. (2026, Phys Imaging Radiat Oncol): 3D U-Net dose engine (6713 MR-linac segments, 130 plans) with MC dropout, mean-variance estimation (MVE) and deep ensembles. After calibration, expected normalized calibration error was 0.14/0.05/0.10. Spearman ρ between uncertainty and MAE: 0.64/0.76/0.67. Thresholds targeting a 10% failure rate (3 mm/3% gamma) accepted 44–77% of segments, with observed failure 7.2–9.5% among accepted. MVE was best-calibrated and cheapest; the ensemble best matched the target failure rate. — [Schneider 2026, PMC13572023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13572023/)
- Borden BJ et al. (2026, J Appl Clin Med Phys): conformal prediction for U-Net segmentation UQ (medical physics precedent). — [doi:10.1002/acm2.70663](https://doi.org/10.1002/acm2.70663) [title only]
- Zaid NRR, Hardiansyah D, Yusufaly T, Rahmim A (2026, PET Clin): argues PBPK models for theranostic digital twins need rigorous verification, validation and uncertainty quantification (VVUQ) before clinical use. — [doi:10.1016/j.cpet.2025.08.004](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cpet.2025.08.004)
- Yousefzadeh-Nowshahr E et al. (2026, Med Phys): global sensitivity analysis (Kucherenko method, UQLab) of a 15-parameter PSMA PBPK model in 10 patients. Kidney AD 4.2 Gy [3.0–7.2] with SD 0.5 Gy; tumour 10.1 Gy [2.0–26.8] with SD 1.7 Gy. Ignoring parameter correlations inflated AD uncertainty by a factor of 1.2 [1.0–1.8]. — [doi:10.1002/mp.70746](https://doi.org/10.1002/mp.70746)
- Out-of-domain failures relevant to OOD gating: Ha 2026 (90Y-trained model on 177Lu: 657% error) — [doi:10.1088/1361-6560/ae5375](https://doi.org/10.1088/1361-6560/ae5375); Ennassiri 2026 (OOD low-density medium) — [doi:10.1088/2057-1976/aea427](https://doi.org/10.1088/2057-1976/aea427)
- Weighted conformal prediction for covariate shift (dose-response, non-radiation): arXiv:2409.20412 (baseline method reference). — [arXiv:2409.20412](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.20412)

### Inferences
- **Recipe for IRSA:** (1) a physics-analytic baseline (line/volume source × BAF × measured T_eff); (2) a learned residual correction; (3) split-conformal calibration on held-out phantom and patient data, with patient-level (not reading-level) exchangeability. Use maximum-type aggregation over the time course for the conservative "never exceed" guarantee (cf. Brodeur 2026). (4) Abstain and fall back to generic rules when ensemble or MVE uncertainty exceeds a threshold tuned to a target failure rate (cf. Schneider 2026), or when inputs fall outside the convex hull of training features (activity, BMI, nuclide, detector site, time since administration).
- The IRSA protocol endpoint "uncertainty-interval coverage" matches conformal practice. Plan for group-conditional coverage (by nuclide, detector site and BMI band), because marginal coverage can hide under-coverage in subgroups such as high-retention patients, who are exactly the safety-relevant tail.
- Conformal guarantees assume exchangeability. Home behaviour (distance and occupancy) is a distribution shift that the detector cannot observe. Coverage claims should be scoped to the "dose rate at specified distance" quantity, not to the realized caregiver dose, unless validated against caregiver dosimeters (as IRSA plans).

### Gaps
- No 2026 MICCAI, NeurIPS or ICML paper applying conformal or OOD detection to radionuclide dosimetry or external-exposure surrogates was found.
- Numerical coverage results of arXiv:2611.14438 were not available in the abstract.

## 5. Theranostic "digital twins" and PBPK for predicting post-discharge activity and excretion; home excretion/contamination modelling

### Takeaway
PBPK-based theranostic digital twins are an active 2025–2026 field (Munich/TUM–Ulm–Würzburg groups; Rahmim/UBC). Work focuses on organ and tumour absorbed dose and on parameter uncertainty, not on predicting whole-body retention for radiation protection or on modelling home excretion and contamination. PBPK could, in principle, give IRSA a mechanistic prior for retention (renal function → T_eff), updated by the wearable's data (Bayesian assimilation). No published implementation of this was found.

### Cited Findings
- Abdollahi H, Yousefirizi F, Shiri I, ..., Rahmim A (2024, Theranostics; baseline): "Theranostic digital twins: concept, framework and roadmap." — [PMC11209714](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11209714/) [title only]

- Yousefzadeh-Nowshahr E et al. (2026, Med Phys): GSA of a whole-body PSMA PBPK model fitted to day 1 and day 4 post-therapy SPECT/CT in 10 patients (details in Section 4). — [doi:10.1002/mp.70746](https://doi.org/10.1002/mp.70746)
- Zaid NRR et al. (2026, PET Clin): VVUQ of PBPK models for theranostic digital twins. — [doi:10.1016/j.cpet.2025.08.004](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cpet.2025.08.004)

- van der Gaag S et al. (2026, Clin Transl Sci): [18F]piflufolastat PET for PBPK prediction of tissue distribution and therapy optimization. — [PMC13177845](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13177845/) [title only]

- Yazdani 2026 review: DT modelling can infer results "from sparsely available patient data", reducing imaging time points. — [Yazdani 2026](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13549228/)

- Kurth J et al. (2018, EJNMMI Res; baseline), "External radiation exposure, excretion, and effective half-life in 177Lu-PSMA-targeted therapies". — [EJNMMI Res 2018](https://ejnmmires.springeropen.com/articles/10.1186/s13550-018-0386-4) [title only]

### Inferences
- **Most valuable modelling for IRSA:** a 2–3 compartment retention model (fast renal and slow tumour/bone components), individualized by on-body measurements in a Bayesian filter, with PBPK-informed priors (eGFR, tumour burden from PSMA/SSTR PET). Full-body PBPK is likely overkill for radiation-protection outputs.
- **Excretion as a confounder:** excretion and contamination are out of scope for an external detector, but voiding events produce step drops in the on-body signal, especially for torso detectors near the bladder. The model must treat these as state changes, not noise. The same signal could log voiding-related activity loss (an inference, not demonstrated in the literature).

### Gaps
- No 2025–2026 study was found that models home excretion or contamination pathways (toilet, laundry, surfaces) after 177Lu or 131I, or that couples PBPK to radiation-protection outputs.
- No study was found that combines wearable measurements with a PBPK digital twin.

## 6. Simplified physics alternatives (point/line source, AAPM/IAEA/NRC patient-release calculations, occupancy factors): how much would an AI surrogate add?

### Takeaway
Regulatory methods (NRC RG 8.39 / NUREG-1556 Vol. 9 lineage) use an unattenuated point source, occupancy factor 0.25 at 1 m (T_phys >1 d) and physical half-life. All three are conservative. Measured evidence quantifies the conservatism as roughly 27–37% from body attenuation (BAF 0.63–0.73) and ~3–4× from physical vs effective half-life for 177Lu (much more for 131I ablation). Most of the accuracy gain is available from measured T_eff plus a body-attenuation and line-source correction. An MC/AI surrogate would mainly add accuracy at short distances (<0.5 m, e.g., bed-sharing, child holding), for non-uniform biodistribution, and for inverting the on-body detector signal. Its marginal value over a well-calibrated semi-analytic model is unproven and should be tested head-to-head in IRSA's validation.

### Cited Findings
- **NRC RG 8.39 Revision 1** (issued April 2020) is a "Phase 1" update covering patient instructions and breastfeeding tables. The dosimetric equations, methodologies and tables are to be updated in Phase 2. Rev. 1 retains the activity-based (1.1), dose-rate-based (1.2) and patient-specific calculation (1.3) release pathways. — [NRC ADAMS ML19232A081](https://forms.nrc.gov/docs/ML1923/ML19232A081.pdf) [snippet only]
- Occupancy factor in NRC methodology: 0.25 at 1 m when T_phys > 1 d (1.0 if < 1 d). An NRC comment document records stakeholder opposition to raising it to 0.75 in a proposed update. — [UCSF PSMA conference poster 2024](https://psmaconference.ucsf.edu/sites/g/files/tkssra8946/f/wysiwyg/resources/posters/2024%20Demystifying%20patient%20release%20criteria.pdf); [NRC ML19154A388](https://www.nrc.gov/docs/ML1915/ML19154A388.pdf) [snippet only]
- SNMMI 2026 abstract (Swinson et al.) confirms OF = 0.25 and physical half-life in current US 177Lu release practice: TEDE (mSv) ≈ 0.576 × DR(mR/h at 1 m). — [JNM 67 suppl 1:26158](https://jnm.snmjournals.org/content/67/supplement_1/26158)
- Point-source conservatism: NRC RG 8.39 ignores body attenuation, redistribution and excretion. Calculated dose rates exceed patient measurements regardless of source model (Willegaignon; Yi). Line-source models (Siegel; Broggio) are reasonable for Tc-99m, F-18 and I-131. — [Ierace 2026](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13638693/)
- Body attenuation factor at 1 m: 0.729 (131I, MC voxel phantom) and 0.628 (FDG) — [Cortez 2026](https://doi.org/10.1088/1361-6498/ae80d4). Self-absorption factor 0.71 ± 0.04 (F-18, phantom measurement) — [Ierace 2026](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13638693/)
- Published 131I Γ_H*(10) values vary from −17% to +22% around the MC representative value: constant choice alone introduces ~20% uncertainty. — [Cortez 2026](https://doi.org/10.1088/1361-6498/ae80d4)
- Effective vs physical half-life for 177Lu: −54% to −74% median deviation; 90th-percentile conservative T½eff of 2.0–4.0 d by compound. — [Schütze 2026](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13046430/)
- Individualized generic rules (UK) derived from retention + dose-rate + social-contact models achieve family doses ~0.04 mSv per 4 weeks — [Levart 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12595148/). Alternative of individualized 6 h/24 h dose-rate-based recommendations was noted in the same source.
- Pre-therapy imaging regression (111In-SRS → next-day 1-m dose rate) achieved only r = 0.56–0.63 — [Takai 2024](https://doi.org/10.1007/s12149-024-01962-0). Imaging-only prediction leaves substantial unexplained variance, which supports direct measurement of T_eff (the wearable's main contribution).
- IAEA Safety Reports Series No. 63 (patient release after RPT, 2009) and AAPM guidance were not retrieved in this session (see Gaps).

### Inferences
- **Error budget for a contact's dose estimate** (inferred from the cited data):
  - Behaviour (distance/time actually spent): unknown and likely the largest term (factor ≥2). Family contact times in Levart were 39.5 h at 1 m and 19.9 h at <1 m per 4 weeks, highly individual.
  - Kinetics (T_eff): range 0.35–6.65 d (Schütze), up to ~10× variation, which IRSA's wearable directly measures.
  - Dose-rate constant: ~±20% (Cortez).
  - Body attenuation: ~30% (BAF 0.63–0.73).
  - Source geometry at <0.5 m: point vs line vs volume differences, largest at short distance (Ierace).
  - An MC/AI surrogate addresses only the last two or three terms, so its *marginal* benefit is modest relative to measuring T_eff and asking about or estimating occupancy.
- **Recommended validation design:** a three-arm comparison on the same patients and caregivers: (a) NRC/RG 8.39 default; (b) semi-analytic (line source × BAF × measured T_eff); (c) IRSA MC surrogate. Endpoints: bias and LoA vs caregiver dosimeters and reference survey meters. This would directly answer whether the AI component adds accuracy.
- **Borrowable methods:**
  - Schütze-style 90th-percentile T_eff as a conservative fallback when the device's T_eff is not yet identifiable (early hours).
  - Cortez/Ierace BAF and self-absorption factors as analytic priors.
  - Bailey's continuous-logging architecture and step-artefact handling.
  - Levart's 4-week continuous caregiver TLD protocol as the reference-standard design.
  - Split-conformal and uncertainty-threshold gating (Brodeur 2026; Schneider 2026) for the fallback rule.
  - GATE 10 for training-data generation.

### Gaps
- I could not retrieve the text of NUREG-1556 Vol. 9 Rev. 3 or RG 8.39 Rev. 1 Section 1.3 to verify exact occupancy-factor wording, or whether a Phase 2 draft (possibly including a ~2.2 mR/h at 1 m pathway) has been issued.
- IAEA SRS-63 and AAPM task-group release guidance were not retrieved. EU/German binding 177Lu discharge criteria are reported as absent (Schütze 2026), but specific national dose-rate discharge thresholds were not verified here.
- No study was found that directly compares an AI/MC surrogate against semi-analytic models for third-party external dose after RPT, so the "added accuracy" of IRSA's surrogate is unquantified in the literature.
